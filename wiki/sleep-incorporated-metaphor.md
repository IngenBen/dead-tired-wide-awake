Four key hormones are "executives" — adenosine, melatonin, cortisol, dopamine. Supporting hormones are "managers." Neurotransmitters are "workers." The book's central organising metaphor (S2 model). Sleep Incorporated makes only one thing — a good night's sleep — and it's an *output*: the CEO can't place an order for it or reach in and operate the machinery. What the CEO can do is send three signals across the day (Activating, Calming, Timing) so the company produces the result on its own. Four hormones function as the top-level decision-makers (the *levers* the signals act through) whose alignment (or misalignment) cascades through the whole system: sleep happens only when the executives are aligned. Managers (supporting hormones) follow the executives; workers (neurotransmitters) follow the managers. Sleeping pills are "outside consultants" who bypass the hierarchy without addressing it. Menopause is a manager-level problem executive alignment alone can't fully override. The metaphor is introduced in ch04 and threaded through every subsequent science chapter.

Since the thesis pass (2026-09-24) the metaphor is the first of three layers: Sleep Incorporated says *what has to happen* (executives aligned at bedtime; misalignment is the diagnosis), [[Calming and Activating Systems]] is the daily decision tool, and training is what consistent practice builds. The company is not broken; it has never been trained to run well. The book closes on the same image: the CEO now has a training program to align the executives and turn the company around.
