The book's single organising model. Sleep Incorporated makes only one thing, and it is an output: the CEO can't place an order for it or work the machinery. What the CEO can do is set the shifts. Each of the four executives has its own job and its own shift, and sleep follows when they work together. Influence flows downhill: managers mostly follow the executives and workers follow the managers, which is why the four are the leverage points and why the reader doesn't need to understand the rest.

**Shifts.** Through the day the Wake Executives run the shift; in the evening they stand down and hand over to the Sleep Executives, who hold the night until the morning. The three jobs map onto this: the body clock is the roster, the calming system is the handover, and the disruptors are the wrong executives called in at the wrong time (both sides). Clocking on and off covers all four executives; "going home" and "leaving the building" fit only the wake side and are used only where a passage is about cortisol or dopamine.

**Diagnosis without defect.** The executives are good staff, working hard, who were never told when their shifts start and end. What fails to turn up is the handover, not the staff. This carries the one-liner "Not broken, just not well trained" (ch04, ch20) and puts the problem where the CEO can act on it (ch10: not the problem, but the solution).

**What the company does that nothing else in the book does** (from the 2026-11-12 review in `derived/core-model.md`): one picture of the whole system; permission not to understand all of it; leverage; indirect control; sleep as an output of the whole day; opposing forces (tired but wired); timing as shifts; ownership without blame; diagnosis without defect; why quick fixes fail (consultants bypass the hierarchy); somewhere to put exceptions at the manager level (menopause, appetite managers); and the labels and structure (Sleep and Wake Executives, the title).

**How the reader runs it.** Not by hand. Habits send the company signals, activating or calming, and the same signals every day train the executives their shifts. That lever and its labels belong to [[Calming and Activating Systems]]; the thesis as a whole is on [[Sleep as a system]].
