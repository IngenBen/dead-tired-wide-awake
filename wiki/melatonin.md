Melatonin tells the body it's time to prepare for sleep; it does not force sleep. Its rise in the evening creates the sleep window — the brief period when adenosine pressure plus melatonin signal plus falling cortisol align to make sleep easy. The window is approximately 30–60 minutes at its steepest rise.

Three things disrupt the signal: evening light (especially short-wavelength screen light), irregular sleep schedule (the system loses its reference point), and hyperarousal/stress (the system is running too hot to listen).

In Ch6, supplements are presented as the wrong tool for a missed window or a weak overnight signal (habits are the fix), and as useful when sleep is on the wrong schedule: jet lag, a new roster, a clock set in the wrong place. Most over-the-counter doses are far above natural levels and risk next-morning carryover.

Supplements have two uses with different timing: a sleep aid for tonight (about an hour before the sleep you want) and a clock-shifter for good (early evening, hours before the body's own melatonin rise, with morning light). Melatonin helps most when the body clock's wake signal is the obstacle and least when stress is. Detail: derived/explainers/melatonin-dose-and-timing.md.
