canonical: Eating within 3 hours of bed raises body temperature and activates the digestive system — the same conditions that prevent sleep onset. The body needs to cool and the activating system needs to stand down. A 3-hour gap is the practical minimum.
---

The apparent contradiction: a heavy meal can feel like it promotes sleepiness ("food coma"), but the sleep that follows is lower quality and more fragmented. The sleepiness is real; the restoration is reduced.

Hunger at bedtime is the other failure mode: an empty stomach that's genuinely uncomfortable triggers cortisol, working against the same sleep onset conditions. A small snack is better than lying awake hungry — but if this becomes regular, use the incremental method to move dinner earlier until the gap closes.

Food composition at bedtime: some evidence for carbohydrates supporting REM and protein supporting deep sleep, but neither finding is strong enough for prescriptive advice.
