canonical: The book's diagnosis of persistent poor sleep. The four executives are working against each other, typically at bedtime, when the Sleep Executives (adenosine, melatonin) can't take over because the Wake Executives (cortisol, dopamine) are still at work. The healthy state is the executives working together. The cause is that they were never well trained in their shifts.

The diagnosis, in company terms. Not a deficiency, disease or personal flaw, but good staff on the wrong shifts, pulling in opposite directions. The vicious cycle is explicit: sleep loss raises cortisol into the next day, which makes the next night harder. This page names *what* goes wrong; [[Sleep as a system]] names *why* (never well trained) and *what to do* (habits that send regular signals, activating or calming).

"Tired but wired", exhausted but unable to sleep, is how working against each other feels: adenosine is high (the pressure is real), but cortisol, dopamine or both are still at work. The book uses the phrase in ch01, ch03, ch18, ch21 and ch23.
