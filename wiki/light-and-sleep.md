canonical: Light is the primary circadian signal. Morning light (bright, consistent timing) sets the body clock. Evening light (particularly from screens at close range) suppresses melatonin. Bedroom darkness during sleep prevents arousal. Blue light matters less than content at typical TV viewing distances.

Light operates on two timescales for sleep: the circadian timescale (morning light sets the clock, the effects show up that evening) and the immediate timescale (evening/bedroom light suppresses the melatonin signal in real time).

Natural outdoor light is dramatically more intense than indoor light — even an overcast sky provides enough photons to trigger the cortisol awakening response. Indoor lighting, phone screens, and ceiling lights cannot replicate this.

The blue light debate: at close range (phone screen, 30cm), blue light is significant enough to suppress melatonin. At TV distance (3m), lux drops below the melatonin-suppression threshold of ~17 lux. Content (dopamine/cortisol engagement) is the more important factor for TV viewing.
