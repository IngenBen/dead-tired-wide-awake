canonical: When sleep isn't coming and frustration is rising, get up. Move to a dim room. Do something only just interesting enough to stop self-directed thinking — not interesting enough to spike dopamine or cortisol. Return to bed when genuinely drowsy. Works on two timescales – tonight (breaks the frustration loop) and over time (rewrites the bed-wakefulness association).
---

The routine addresses two hormonal problems simultaneously. Staying in bed anxious spikes cortisol (stress response) and can perpetuate dopamine-driven thought spirals. Getting up removes the pressure to sleep (ACT-i principle) and prevents further conditioning of bed as a wakefulness context (CBT-i principle).

Activity calibration is specific: just enough stimulation to exit self-directed thought, not enough to call the Wake Executives back. TV (sitcoms, mild comedies, cartoons > dramas > thrillers). Reading. Music. The author found cartoons optimal — interesting enough, but content he wouldn't normally choose.

In the final program version: try the body scan meditation first (ch20). If unsuccessful, proceed to this routine.

Blue light footnote: TV at 3 metres registers ~5 lux; phone at 30cm registers 25–50 lux. Content matters more than light at this distance.
