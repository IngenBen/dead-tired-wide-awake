The core principle: the morning routine is not really about the morning — it is about the following evening. Morning light at a consistent time sets the circadian countdown; everything downstream (cortisol arc, adenosine accumulation, melatonin timing) runs from that fixed anchor.

Sleep-ins — even partial — disrupt the anchor. The cortisol awakening response fires later; melatonin rises later; the sleep window arrives later. The knock-on effect can run for several days. Rule: wake time does not move, even after a bad night. Nap (short, not before mid-morning) if needed, but protect the anchor.

Natural light is dramatically brighter than indoor light — sufficient even on an overcast day. Bright-light therapy lamps are a fallback for those unable to go outside.
