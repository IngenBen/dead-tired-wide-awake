Two mechanisms link caffeine to poor sleep:

1. **Adenosine blockade:** Caffeine doesn't produce energy — it masks the signal that tiredness has built. When it wears off, the accumulated adenosine floods through (the afternoon slump). Late coffee means adenosine is still being blocked when the Sleep Executives need it to land.

2. **Cortisol morning window:** Drinking coffee in the first 60–90 minutes after waking may interfere with the natural cortisol awakening response and increase afternoon energy crashes (referenced to Huberman; book acknowledges developing research).

The self-reinforcing loop: residual adenosine on waking → reach for coffee → blocks adenosine signal → less deep sleep → more residual adenosine → reach for coffee.

Technique: incremental reduction, not cold turkey. Start by moving the last coffee of the day 5–10 minutes earlier; pause until normal; repeat.
