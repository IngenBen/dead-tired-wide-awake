canonical: The state in which the four key hormones fail to follow their correct daily arc — particularly at bedtime, when sleep executives (adenosine, melatonin) cannot gain control because wake executives (cortisol, dopamine) remain elevated. Misaligned is the book's only diagnosis word and describes the hormones; the cause is that the systems keeping them aligned are not well trained.
---

The book's diagnosis of persistent poor sleep: not a deficiency, disease or personal flaw, but a failure of hormonal timing. The vicious cycle is explicit — sleep loss disrupts the hormones, disrupted hormones worsen sleep, and so on. Misalignment names *what* is going wrong; [[Sleep as a system]] names *why* (the body clock and calming system are not well trained) and *what to do* (train them through daily habits, mostly during the day).

"Tired but wired" — exhausted but unable to sleep — is the felt signature of misalignment: adenosine is high (the pressure is real), but cortisol and/or dopamine are also high (the wake executives won't stand down).
