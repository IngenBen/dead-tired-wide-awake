Three mechanisms converge at 2–3am (relative to a typical 10–11pm bedtime): (1) melatonin is at or just past its peak (usually 2–4 h after sleep onset, ~12:30–2:30am for a 10:30pm bedtime) and production begins to tail off, weakening the sleep-timing signal; (2) deep sleep is front-loaded into the first half of the night, so adenosine has been largely spent and is "still working, but with less force"; (3) cortisol is only just beginning its pre-dawn rise.

For someone who sleeps well these shifts are imperceptible — cortisol is low enough that the fading Sleep Executives still hold. For someone who has slept badly for years and carries elevated cortisol through the night, the small uptick at 2–3am is enough: the Wake Executive overpowers the weakened Sleep Executives, often producing a racing mind and full wakefulness. Sleep is usually done for the night.

The most common aggravator: reaching for the phone at this moment. Even a brief glance provides a dopamine hit that fully activates both Wake Executives, making return to sleep nearly impossible.
