The book's central thesis (this is the thesis page). Poor sleep is not a personal trait or a fault in the person; it is the result of daily inputs, and it responds to daily training the same way fitness does. The thesis runs from the belief set up in ch01 (bad sleep as part of who you are), through the reveal in ch03, the one-liner in ch04, the hand-over into the program in ch09–10, the payoff in ch20, to the restatement in ch26.

**Three layers.** *[SUPERSEDED 2026-11-12: see `derived/core-model.md`. Company = the model (with its shifts), signals = the lever, training = the change process.]* [[Sleep Incorporated metaphor]] says *what has to happen* (four executives aligned at bedtime; misalignment is the diagnosis). [[Calming and Activating Systems]] is *the daily decision tool* (activating through the day, calming through the evening, timing between). Training is *what consistent practice builds*: a trained body clock and a strong calming system.

**Vocabulary rules (thesis pass, 2026-09-24).** *[Misaligned part SUPERSEDED 2026-11-11: misaligned retired as a concept. The broken / fix / labels rules still stand.]* *Misaligned* is the only diagnosis word and describes the hormones; *not well trained* describes the systems that keep them aligned. Older diagnosis words — well-regulated, regulation, balance, "fault in the handover" — were replaced with training words. *Broken* describes the sleep, never the person. *Fix* is the language of the wrong approach (quick fixes); the goal is built, trained or rebuilt. Labels describe the sleep, not the person ("someone who sleeps badly", not "a poor sleeper"); lines that state and reject the belief ("I'm just a bad sleeper") are kept deliberately.

**Analogy versus literal fitness.** The comparison is to how fitness is built (small, consistent, over time), never an instruction to exercise more. Where the book discusses real exercise (ch11 tracker, ch18 walk, ch22) the analogy is kept out, and ch22 separates the two explicitly. The term "sleep fit" was considered and deliberately not coined; the book uses plain training language instead.
