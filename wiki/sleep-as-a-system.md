The book's central thesis (this is the thesis page). Poor sleep is not a trait or a fault in the person. It is a company of good staff that has never been well trained, and it responds to daily signals. The thesis runs from the belief set up in ch01, through the reveal in ch03, the model and one-liner in ch04, the hand-over into the program in ch09 and ch10, the payoff in ch20, to the restatement in ch26. The full model, the pub version and the decision history live in `derived/core-model.md`.

**Three concepts, each answering a different question.**

| Question | Concept |
|---|---|
| What makes sleep? | **The company** (the model): dozens of hormones, no one thing that makes them all behave, so attention goes to the four executives at the top. See [[Sleep Incorporated metaphor]] and [[Four executives]]. |
| What has to happen? | **Its shifts**: the executives clock on and off at the right times, with a clean handover between the day shift and the night shift. |
| How do I do that? | **Signals** (the lever): habits that send regular signals, activating or calming. See [[Calming and Activating Systems]]. |
| How does that change anything? | **Training** (the change process): send the right signals every day and the executives learn their shifts. |

The bridge between the last two: repeated signals are the training. One signal changes little.

**The three jobs** are what the signals achieve, not a separate list. Train the body clock (the roster: every executive on and off at the right times), build the calming system (the handover: the wake side clocks off so the sleep side can take over and hold the night), remove the disruptors (calling the wrong executives in at the wrong time, on either side: late coffee, phones and sugar call the wake side back in; long or late naps and sleep-ins disturb the sleep side). See [[The Program]].

**Fitness is an illustration only.** It appears in ch03 for "no shortcut, not overnight", in ch20 (echoed in ch21 and ch22) for the calming system as something built like a muscle, and in ch26 as a quick comparison. It never describes what sleep is, the executives never train like athletes, and "sleep fit" is not coined. Where the book discusses real exercise (ch18 walk, ch22) the image is kept out.

**Vocabulary rules.** The executives work *together* or *against each other*; "aligned / misaligned" is retired as a concept (2026-11-11) and no longer appears in the chapters. *Broken* describes the sleep, never the person; *fix* is the language of the wrong approach. Labels describe the sleep, not the person; lines that state and reject the belief ("I'm just a bad sleeper") are kept on purpose. "Trained" is the book's change word; "practice" only as plain description; no "reps" and no "out of practice" (it assumes the reader once slept well). Clocking on and off covers all four executives; wake-side-only wording ("go home", "leaves the building") is kept to passages about cortisol or dopamine.
