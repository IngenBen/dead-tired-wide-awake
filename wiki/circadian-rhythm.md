The circadian rhythm is not fixed hardware — it is set and maintained by behavioural inputs, principally the time of first light exposure after waking. A consistent wake time with morning light anchors the system; variable wake times (especially sleeping in) shift the entire downstream sequence — later cortisol rise, later melatonin, later sleep window — creating the conditions for insomnia even on nights when nothing obvious goes wrong.

Whether sleep comes depends on two forces together: sleep pressure, which builds while awake, and the clock's wake signal, which peaks in the evening wake maintenance zone before habitual bedtime. This is why a late clock is hard to move abruptly at home (a much earlier bedtime lands in that zone), while jet lag resolves on its own (daylight at the destination resets the clock). Supplemental melatonin works on the clock signal; see [[Melatonin]] and derived/explainers/melatonin-dose-and-timing.md. (The two-process model: Borbély 2016; daytime sleep cut short by the clock's alerting signal: Sack 2007.)

The program's Phase 3 habits ("Train your body clock": can't-sleep, morning and evening routines) exist entirely to set and protect this clock.
