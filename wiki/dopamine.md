canonical: One of two Wake Executives (alongside cortisol). Where cortisol keeps you alert through urgency (anything that feels like it needs immediate action, threat or not; Ch07), dopamine keeps you alert through motivation, anticipation and novelty-seeking — responding to opportunity. At night, screen content and social feeds keep it running, blocking the Sleep Executives' takeover and potentially pushing the sleeper past the sleep window.
---

Dopamine is the quietest disruptor. Unlike cortisol (which has an obvious felt effect), dopamine-driven engagement feels pleasant and harmless — curiosity, interest, mild amusement. This makes it easy to miss.

The "calm-good vs hyped-good" distinction is the book's key framing: wholesome curiosity content feels calmer than outrage, but both deliver dopamine and keep the Wake Executive active. The mechanism is identical regardless of content quality.

The scrolling feed is engineered around unpredictability — the key dopamine trigger — making it structurally different from most other evening activities.
