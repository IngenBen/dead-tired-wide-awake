Hydration is framed as obstacle removal, not optimisation. Two mechanisms: (1) mild dehydration registers as physiological stress, slightly elevating the cortisol baseline; (2) dehydration impairs the body's ability to shed heat (necessary for sleep onset).

Night-time bathroom trips: the body is designed to suppress the bladder signal during deep sleep. In someone who sleeps badly, with elevated cortisol and light sleep, the signal that most people sleep through becomes loud enough to wake. As sleep quality improves, bathroom trips typically reduce — the author went from multiple trips to once per night that no longer bothered him.

Practical rule: 2L target consumed before evening; taper after dinner (small sips only); don't catch up late (large evening volume disrupts sleep more than mild shortfall).
