canonical: Sleep deprivation both reduces self-control and dysregulates the appetite managers (ghrelin up, leptin down) — increasing cravings, especially for sweet/fatty food, while lowering the willpower to resist them. This "double bind" is why the book chooses incremental change over cold turkey and sequences the willpower-heavy habits (caffeine, sugar) only after sleep has begun to improve.

The book leans on this claim repeatedly to justify its method. Willpower is treated not as a character trait but as a depletable resource that poor sleep never fully recharges (ch14, ch02). On top of that, ch25 adds the appetite mechanism: short sleep raises ghrelin and lowers leptin, and ghrelin specifically nudges cravings toward sugar and fat. The result is a self-reinforcing trap — cravings turned up, willpower turned down, both aimed at the same snack.

This reframes "failure to quit" as biology rather than weakness, and it shapes the program's design in two ways: change is made incrementally (small moves a depleted body can sustain — see [[Incremental habit change]]), and the habits that most demand willpower are deliberately placed late, after earlier habits have lifted sleep enough to refill the tank. The payoff described in ch25 is that cravings become "resistible" once sleep improves — the same loop running in reverse.
