Adenosine is produced from the moment of waking and accumulates steadily through the day. By evening its concentration should be high enough to create real, physical sleep pressure — the sensation of tiredness that pulls you toward bed. During deep (slow-wave) sleep the brain clears it; a full night resets the cycle to near-zero by morning.

Two failure modes appear in the book: (1) caffeine blocking the adenosine signal, masking pressure without clearing it, which then floods through when caffeine wears off (the afternoon slump); (2) fragmented deep sleep preventing clearance, producing morning grogginess and perpetuating the caffeine-dependence loop.

Most people who have slept badly for years have adequate adenosine (pressure is real) — the problem is something overriding or preventing its clearance, not insufficient production.
