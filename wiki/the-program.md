The program is the practical output of the book's science. Rather than a set of categories, the habits now do **three jobs** — which are ch04's three CEO signals (Activating, Calming, Timing) turned into daily action: build the calming system (Calming), stop propping up the activating system late in the day (Activating), and keep the body clock accurate so the swap between the two lands on time (Timing). Twelve habits are introduced across five phases (a set-up phase, then four phases of three habits each):

1. Get set up — tracker, supplements, environment (ch11–ch13)
2. Remove the first three disruptors — caffeine, alcohol, food timing (ch14–ch16; Habits 1–3)
3. Train your body clock — can't-sleep routine, morning routine, evening routine (ch17–ch19; Habits 4–6)
4. Build your nervous system — meditation, napping, exercise (ch20–ch22; Habits 7–9)
5. Remove the final three disruptors — social media, hydration, sugar & food sensitivities (ch23–ch25; Habits 10–12)

Habits are numbered from Phase 2 onward — Habit 1 (Caffeine, ch14) through Habit 12 (Sugar & Food Sensitivities, ch25). The principle throughout: a habit you sustain beats a perfect protocol you abandon. Science-optimal was deliberately sacrificed for life-optimal. The author framed this as 6 weeks to a noticeable change — not fixed, but a clear signal that breaks the "I'm just a bad sleeper" story.
