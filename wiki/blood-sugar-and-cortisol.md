The loop: sugar → glucose spike → insulin overcorrection → glucose dip → cortisol surge → Wake Executive reactivated. When this happens at 2–3am from an evening dessert or snack, the result is waking alert and anxious at the worst point of the night. Many people attribute this to needing the bathroom — but the cortisol surge often precedes and triggers the bathroom awareness.

The daytime version of the loop: repeated sugar hits throughout the day overwork the cortisol response, eventually making the Wake Executive reactive and unreliable — firing when it shouldn't, flat when needed.

Long-term resolution: as sleep improves, sugar cravings reduce naturally because the slumps that drove them reduce. Sugar is addressed last in the program deliberately — earlier habits improve sleep enough to make sugar reduction manageable.
