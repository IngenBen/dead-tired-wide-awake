canonical: The nervous system runs on two settings — an activating system (sympathetic / fight-or-flight, cortisol-driven, alert and ready) and a calming system (parasympathetic / rest-and-digest, cortisol-suppressing, maintenance mode). They are built to take turns: activating through the day, calming at night. Poor sleep almost always comes down to one of two things — a calming system too weak to take over, or the two swapping on the wrong schedule — and both can be trained. Activated is a nervous-system state, not physical movement: you can be still and activated (scrolling, a racing mind) or moving and calm (yoga, a walk).

This is the spine the back half of the book hangs on. In the S2 model, the CEO of Sleep Incorporated guides the system with three signals across the day — **Activating, Calming, Timing** — and ch04 christens the pair "activating / calming" label-only at that point. ch05 then simply *uses* the names (caffeine as a clean activating example). From there the terms run as plain lowercase "activating / calming" language to the end. Verb forms describe inputs (caffeine, curiosity, scrolling *activate* you); the noun describes the trainable system. "wind up / wind down" survives only as descriptive/idiomatic language — it is no longer a coined term. The older "calm-good vs hyped-good" and "top-down / bottom-up" framings have been dissolved into this single vocabulary.

The program's organising idea is the **three jobs** (ch10, ch26), which are ch04's **three signals turned into daily action**: Calming → build the calming system; Activating → stop propping up the activating system late in the day; Timing → keep the body clock accurate so the swap between the two lands on time. Strictly the body clock is a separate system, but it's close enough to picture as three jobs — wind down, stop winding up, keep time — and every habit is one of the three. (This replaced the earlier "three categories.")

The calming system has a physical anatomy: its primary channel is the vagus nerve, directly stimulated by slow, extended exhalation. It is trainable like a muscle — daily 10–15 min sessions build more capacity than infrequent long ones — which is why meditation and somatic movement sit at the centre of the program.
