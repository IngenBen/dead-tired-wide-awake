canonical: The nervous system runs on two settings — an activating system (sympathetic / fight-or-flight, cortisol-driven, alert and ready) and a calming system (parasympathetic / rest-and-digest, cortisol-suppressing, maintenance mode). They are built to take turns: activating through the day, calming at night. Poor sleep almost always comes down to one of two things — a calming system too weak to take over, or the two swapping on the wrong schedule — and both can be trained. Activated is a nervous-system state, not physical movement: you can be still and activated (scrolling, a racing mind) or moving and calm (yoga, a walk).

This is the spine the back half of the book hangs on. The pair first appears as plain language in ch05 (caffeine as a clean activating example); ch04 names only the calming system, as one of the three things retraining the company takes. (Until 2026-11-11 ch04 christened the pair through the CEO's three signals — Activating, Calming, Timing; that framework was retired in favour of the company-culture framing.) From there the terms run as plain lowercase "activating / calming" language to the end. Verb forms describe inputs (caffeine, curiosity, scrolling *activate* you); the noun describes the trainable system. "wind up / wind down" survives only as descriptive/idiomatic language — it is no longer a coined term. The older "calm-good vs hyped-good" and "top-down / bottom-up" framings have been dissolved into this single vocabulary.

The program's organising idea is the **three jobs** (ch04, ch10, ch26), named to match the phase headings: **train the body clock** (Phase 3), **build the calming system** (Phase 4), **remove the disruptors** (Phases 2 and 5). ch04 introduces them in company terms as what retraining the company takes: a reliable body clock so the executives know when to clock on and when to clock off, a strong calming system so they actually do, and a CEO who stops getting in their way. ch10 states them plainly and ch26 recaps them in the same words. Every habit is one of the three. (This replaced the earlier "three categories" and, from 2026-11-11, the "three signals".)

The calming system has a physical anatomy: its primary channel is the vagus nerve, directly stimulated by slow, extended exhalation. It is trainable like a muscle — daily 10–15 min sessions build more capacity than infrequent long ones — which is why meditation and somatic movement sit at the centre of the program.
