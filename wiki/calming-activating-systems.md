canonical: The book's two signals and the two systems they train. The activating system (sympathetic, fight or flight, go-mode) brings the day shift on; the calming system (parasympathetic, rest and digest) is the handover to the night shift and holds the night. They take turns – activating through the day, calming in the evening and at night. Habits send activating or calming signals; one signal changes little, the same signals every day train the system. Bad sleep comes down to a handover that never happens or the two swapping on the wrong schedule, and both can be trained. Activated is a nervous-system state, not movement – you can be still and activated (scrolling, a racing mind) or moving and calm (yoga, a walk).

The lever the back half of the book hangs on. The company is the model ([[Sleep Incorporated metaphor]]); activating and calming are how the reader acts on it. Habits send signals, and the signals are the training. Clock on and handover are the plain-English company version of the same pair; the book runs them as one model, never two.

**Two kinds only.** Timing is *when* the signals are sent (the shifts), not a third signal; the "three signals" (activating / calming / timing) were retired 2026-11-11. A rejection of signalling in the book (ch20 L25) is a rejection of the single in-the-moment signal, not of signals.

**Labels.** In descriptive prose the two are "the activating system" and "the calming system", or the company version (day shift / night shift, the handover). They don't become "side", "the calm", "the activation", "calm takes over" or "the activated state"; the 2026-11-12 sweep removed the last of these (ch06 L29, ch18 L45, ch20 L3, ch20 L51, endnote L885). Plain adjectives that describe a state are fine ("still activated", "you're activated", "go-mode"). Verb forms describe inputs (caffeine, curiosity and scrolling *activate* you); the nouns describe the trainable systems.

**The three jobs** (ch04, ch10, ch26) match the phase headings: train the body clock (Phase 3), build the calming system (Phase 4), remove the disruptors (Phases 2 and 5). In company terms: the roster, a clean handover, and not calling the wrong executives in at the wrong time (both sides; ch21 shows the sleep side). Every habit is one of the three, and each sends an activating or calming signal.

**Anatomy.** The calming system's main channel is the vagus nerve, worked directly by slow breathing and by attending to the body. It builds like a muscle (the ch20 illustration): daily 10–15 minute sessions build more than infrequent long ones, which is why meditation and somatic movement sit at the centre of Phase 4.
