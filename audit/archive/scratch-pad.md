# Ch04 Audit — Beat Analysis
*Sleep Incorporated — Chapter 4*

---

## Stage 1: Beat-by-beat analysis

The chapter has 14 distinct beats. Each is assessed for relevance, weight, and redundancy.

---

**Beat 1 — Introduce the metaphor (paras 1–3)**
> "I'd like you to think about the sleep system as a hierarchy, like a company. I'm going to call it Sleep Incorporated. You're the CEO. / At the top you have 4 key hormones. These are your executives."

Relevant. Load-bearing. This is the chapter's core proposition.

---

**Beat 2 — Scope caveat (paras 4–5)**
> "These aren't every hormone involved in sleep — there are dozens. They're the four you can actually influence..."
> "It's not a complete biological picture — it's something more useful: your 4 best leverage points. Your A-team."

⚠️ **Redundant.** Two paras saying the same thing: these four aren't the full picture, but they're the useful ones. One can go.

Suggested fix — collapse to a single para:

> ~~"These aren't every hormone involved in sleep — there are dozens. They're the four you can actually influence — and as it happens, the four that do most of the work."~~

> ~~"It's not a complete biological picture — it's something more useful: your 4 best leverage points. Your A-team."~~

> **"These aren't every hormone involved in sleep — there are dozens. But these four are the ones you can actually influence. Your best leverage points. Your A-team."**

---

**Beat 3 — "Let's meet them" + two-and-two framing (paras 6–7)**
> "Lets meet them. / Two strongly influence sleep. And two strongly influence awake."

Relevant. Light but necessary as a structural bridge.

⚠️ **Typo:** "Lets" → "Let's"

---

**Beat 4 — The four executives introduced (paras 8–11)**
> Adenosine / Melatonin / Cortisol / Dopamine with one-line descriptions.

Relevant and well-weighted. Each description is tight.

⚠️ **Grammatical drift in the descriptions.** Inconsistent verb tense/person across the four intros:
- "is the force that builds..." ✓
- "Watching the clock and signal your body..." ✗ (gerund + bare infinitive mismatch)
- "Gets you up in the morning, sharpen your focus, and keep you going..." ✗ (should be "sharpens" and "keeps")
- "wired to keep you stimulated and searching" ✓

Suggested fix — Melatonin:

> ~~"Watching the clock and signal your body night has arrived and it's time to sleep."~~

> **"Watching the clock and signalling your body that night has arrived and it's time to sleep."**

Suggested fix — Cortisol:

> ~~"Gets you up in the morning, sharpen your focus, and keep you going through the day."~~

> **"Gets you up in the morning, sharpens your focus, and keeps you going through the day."**

---

**Beat 5 — Managers and workers (paras 12–13)**
> "Below the executives sit the managers... / Below the managers are the workers..."

Relevant. Establishes the hierarchy. Well-paced.

⚠️ **Minor redundancy.** "They almost entirely depend on clear direction from above. In almost all cases, if the executives are aligned..." — same qualifier ("almost entirely" / "in almost all cases") appears twice in adjacent sentences. Cut one.

Suggested fix:

> ~~"And they almost entirely depend on clear direction from above. In almost all cases, if the executives are aligned, the managers follow — and the workers follow the managers."~~

> **"They depend on clear direction from above. If the executives are aligned, the managers follow — and the workers follow the managers."**

---

**Beat 6 — Aligned state (para 14)**
> "When the executives are aligned, the whole company functions..."

Relevant. Short. Earns its place.

---

**Beat 7 — Misaligned state (para 15)**
> "When the executives are not aligned — cortisol still high when it should be falling, dopamine fed all evening when it should be quieting — the managers get conflicting instructions..."

Relevant and load-bearing. The "tired but wired" image is strong. Pulls its weight.

---

**Beat 8 — Sleeping pills as outside consultant (paras 16–18)**
> "This is why most quick fixes are unreliable. A sleeping pill, for instance, is like an outside consultant..."
> "Sometimes it works, sometimes it doesn't. It never fixes the root problem: misaligned executives..."
> "I'm not anti-sleeping pills. Used short-term, they can be the best thing to take the edge off..."

Relevant. The consultant analogy is strong and original. The qualifier (I'm not anti-sleeping pills) is well-placed.

⚠️ **Minor redundancy between paras 16 and 17.** The analogy already implies unreliability; "Sometimes it works, sometimes it doesn't" adds nothing new before the punchline. The "never fixes the root problem" line is the point — cut the preamble.

Suggested fix:

> ~~"Sometimes it works, sometimes it doesn't. It never fixes the root problem: misaligned executives."~~

> **"It never fixes the root problem: misaligned executives."**

---

**Beat 9 — Shower moment / the click (paras 19–22)**
> "So if the executives were the thing to fix — I needed to understand exactly what fixing them looked like... Then one morning, standing in the shower, I drew it on the foggy glass with my finger."
> "That's when it clicked..."

Relevant. This is the chapter's required personal-grounding moment. Specific, load-bearing, in the author's voice. Meets the standard.

⚠️ **Placement concern (flag only — author decision).** Per the style notes, personal grounding should come early. This is the chapter's first specific named personal moment and it sits two-thirds of the way through. The opening is entirely second-person/metaphor. Not fatal — the metaphor opening is strong — but worth the author knowing.

---

**Beat 10 — "I lived the misaligned version" (paras 23–28)**
> "I lived the misaligned version for more than 20 years. / Midnight. Wide awake..."

Relevant. Highly effective. The stacked short clauses mirroring the executive introductions ("Cortisol was still high... Dopamine had been fed... Melatonin was trying to rise... Adenosine was high.") are the chapter's best writing.

---

**Beat 11 — Generalisation + the key insight (paras 29–34)**
> "When sleep starts to fail, the natural instinct is to look for the hormonal culprit... / But in most cases of chronic poor sleep, the hormonal disruption isn't the cause. It's the consequence."

Relevant and conceptually important. Strong reframe.

⚠️ **Buried (flag only — author decision).** This is arguably the chapter's most important clinical insight — hormones as consequence, not cause; the vicious cycle. It arrives after the personal narrative rather than being woven into the framework. The author may want to consider whether it would land harder integrated earlier or whether the current position (after personal experience has earned it) is intentional.

---

**Beat 12 — Exceptions (paras 35–40)**
> Menopause section + rare diseases section.

Both relevant. The menopause section is well-handled and appropriately brief.

⚠️ **Grammatical error:** "In the Sleep Incorporated think of it as managers working from a different set of instructions" — missing noun. Should be "In the Sleep Incorporated **framework**" (or "**metaphor**").

⚠️ **Capitalisation inconsistency:** "specific problems for Managers, or Workers" — "Workers" is capitalised here but lowercase throughout the chapter. Standardise to lowercase.

Suggested fix:

> ~~"There are also some rare diseases which cause specific problems for Managers, or Workers."~~

> **"There are also some rare diseases which cause specific problems for managers, or workers."**

---

**Beat 13 — Summary / CEO callback (paras 41–44)**
> "So here's where you are. / You're the CEO of Sleep Incorporated..."

Relevant. The personal reflection ("I was the CEO who kept calling in outside contractors") connects well to the sleeping-pill beat.

⚠️ **Incomplete verb phrase:** "A system that wants to run well and will." — should be "and **will do so**" or restructured with an em dash: "A system that wants to run well — **and will**."

---

**Beat 14 — Handoff to next four chapters (paras 45–47 + footnote)**
> "What you don't have yet is the detail... That's what the next four chapters are for."

Relevant. Functional. Does the job.

The footnote on adenosine — good voice, honest, appropriately self-deprecating. Keep as-is.
