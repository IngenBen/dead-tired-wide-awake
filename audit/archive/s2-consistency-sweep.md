## Suggested edit — ch09, the "three jobs" paragraph

Your nervous system runs on those two settings, and they're built to take turns — activating through the day, calming at night. Bad sleep is almost always a fault in that handover. Either the calming system has grown too weak to take over, or the two are swapping on the wrong schedule.

So the habits do three jobs**, the three signals from Chapter 4 turned into daily action**. Train the body clock. Build the calming system. Stop activation at the wrong times. Keep time, wind down, stop winding up. Every habit ahead is one of those three.

The program runs in order — twelve habits, each building on the last. Ready for you to work through at your own pace.

*Why: ch04 now names the CEO's three signals — activating, calming, timing. ch09's three jobs are
the same three turned into action (keep time = timing; build the calming system = calming; stop
activation = activating), but a reader has to infer that. The added clause names the link, so "three
jobs" reads as the payoff of "three signals" rather than a fresh trio. Order kept as written; the
felt-sense triad "keep time, wind down, stop winding up" is untouched. Light-touch — flag, your call.*
