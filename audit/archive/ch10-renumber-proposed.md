# PART 4 – The habits

Everything that follows in Part 4 of this book is based on the three jobs from the last chapter: training the body clock, building the calming system, and stopping activation at the wrong times. The habits come one at a time.


I was the problem, and I was the solution. I was the CEO of Sleep Incorporated, which meant it was mine to fix.

That felt heavy but understanding it was actually a relief. I'd spent years looking for something outside myself to fix it. A pill. A routine I'd read about somewhere. A YouTube expert with the one thing I was missing. None of it stuck, because none of it had addressed the whole company.

Once I stopped waiting to be rescued and started running the company myself, I got to work on a training program.

For most of my life I'd been stuck on the activated setting — wound up, switched on, a system that never clocked off. What was missing was the other one: a calming system strong enough to take over at night.

You met those two settings back in Chapter 4: they're built to take turns, and bad sleep is almost always a fault in the handover — a calming system too weak to take over, or the two swapping on the wrong schedule.

So the habits do three jobs, the three signals from Chapter 4 turned into daily action. Train the body clock. Build the calming system. Stop activation at the wrong times. Every habit in the chapters ahead is one of those three. Know each habit's job and you'll see how it works toward a good night's sleep.

The program runs in order — twelve habits, each building on the last. Ready for you to work through at your own pace.

Each habit is based on solid, well-established sleep research — the same science we walked through in the last four chapters.

What I've done is curate. Determined the sequence. Determined how much to do and when. Worked out what can flex and still give results. And most importantly built it to fit into a modern lifestyle - full-time job, family, exercise, and the general busyness of a life.

I could easily give you twice as many habits, and they'd all have research behind them. But that's not a program. That's a list. And a list is easy to close and never look at again.

Massage, for example, didn't make the cut. The research is solid. But the cost and time away from the kids' dinner and bed routine, just wasn't realistic for me.

The question for each habit was the same: what does science say is ideal, what's realistically achievable most of the time, and do I still get results if I aim for that?

I very deliberately sacrificed science-optimal for life-optimal. Because a habit you sustain beats a perfect protocol you abandon in a couple of weeks. The habits in this program are the ones that survived that test.

Your schedule and family life won't be exactly the same as mine. But I hope to share what I did, give you an understanding why, and encourage you to be imperfect and adjust to suit your circumstances.

Some of these habits you might already be doing. If so, great — cross them off and take the win. For the rest, go slowly. Introduce one or two habits at a time. If they feel easy, add a couple more. If not, pause and work on these until they do. Slow changes that stick beat fast ones that don't.

A fair question before you start is: how long? The honest answer is it depends on where you're starting and how quickly you move. The program runs in phases. Move at roughly a phase a week and you could be through them in six weeks; if you already have some of these habits in place, then maybe faster. Go slower and that's fine too — this isn't a race.

But the moment worth waiting for isn't the end. It's the first time the change becomes undeniable — not "I think I feel better", but "something has definitely shifted". For me, that arrived a week or two after those habits had bedded in — the first nights my calming system did the work on its own, without me forcing it. That's what breaks the old story: I'll never be a good sleeper. Once it comes, your motivation to finish the remaining habits takes care of itself.

One last thing before Phase 1 begins.

I strongly recommend tracking whatever you're working on, with regular self-check-ins. The easiest way is the app at www.sleepbuilders.com.au. It's free, so I'd really encourage you to use it. It'll help you navigate the program, track where you're at, and prompt those check-ins. If you're like me, I didn't always do everything, every day. I needed reminding and reinforcement.

If an app isn't for you, paper works just as well. A simple list, phases marked off, habits ticked as they land. A few notes-to-self about progress. What matters isn't how — it's that you do it. Writing things down is a simple and powerful way to help with commitment.

Either way - app or paper - here's the thing I'd encourage most of all. People are far more likely to see the program to the end if they are doing it with other people — working the same habits, answering questions and helping each other through challenges. It really is worth joining the community — it's called **Sleep Builders**, and you'll find it at the same url, www.sleepbuilders.com.au.

That's it. You now know everything you need to know before we begin. There's nothing left to do but turn to Chapter 11, and let's get to work.
