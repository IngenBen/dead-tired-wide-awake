## Concept 1 — Irritability texture + detachment (Ch01)

Expands the existing masking/irritability paragraph and adds a detachment beat. The
standalone line "But deeply exhausted." is removed and replaced by a reworded close to
the irritability paragraph ("Trying to cope was deeply exhausting.").

**Insertion point:** current Ch01 paragraphs 7–11 (from "Despite being a glass-half-full…"
through the handbrake line).

**Replace with:**

> Despite being a glass-half-full kind of person, I was often depressed or moody. I
> learned to mask it. Lacking willpower and motivation, I pushed through on self-talk
> alone. Craving sugar and caffeine. Getting irritated by things that really shouldn't
> have bothered me - I was good at filtering what came out… most of the time. It rarely
> boiled over into anger. It usually surfaced as short snappy comments, or more typically
> withdrawing myself before I said something I would regret.
>
> Trying to cope was deeply exhausting.
>
> Other times, when I wasn't dealing with irritability, I just felt detached. Like I was
> on a different wavelength. I'd say or do something I thought might get a laugh, and it
> would land as weird, or just plain mean. Later I'd look back and wonder who that person
> was.
>
> All the masking, the pushing through, the filtering, the disconnection - and all the
> while life never stopped. It was completely overwhelming. Keeping up felt like driving
> down the highway with the handbrake on.

**Notes:**
- Original "But deeply exhausted." fragment relocated + reworded to "Trying to cope was
  deeply exhausting." — a complete thought that closes the irritability beat on its own.
- Watch a soft echo: "exhausting" here vs "overwhelming" two paragraphs down. Reads fine;
  if distinct hits are wanted, lean this line into effort ("took everything I had") and
  let "overwhelming" carry the exhaustion.

> I had recurring dreams, too. The setting was always different, but the theme never

## Concept 5 — "I wanted it to be one thing" (Ch01)

New paragraph. The wish for a discrete, nameable, curable cause — a fixable villain —
distinct from the existing "one thing / silver bullet" motif (which is about hunting for a
single tip or product, not a diagnosis). Sets up the book's actual answer (it was the
whole system, not one thing) and plays into the Ch25:64 inversion of the motif.

**Insertion point:** Ch01 resignation stretch. Sits AFTER Concept 3a (recurring dreams)
and BEFORE "Maybe it was genetic…". Order gives: self-doubt ("just me") → dreams deepen it
→ wish for an external fixable cause (hope flares) → genetic resignation (collapse).

**Insert:**

> I so desperately wanted it to be one thing. A vitamin deficiency. A parasite. Some
> disease with a name and a cure — something I could point to and fix. A single villain to
> defeat. But there was never anything to find.

**Notes:**
- Cut the author's original trailing second "one thing" (draft repeated it); "a single
  villain to defeat" carries the idea without the echo — same dedup logic applied to
  Concept 2's "another year."
- Soft echo to watch: "desperately" sits near "Exhausted and desperate" (Ch01:27) and
  Ch03:7 "exhausted and desperate." Not adjacent, reads fine; if protecting "desperate"
  for the desert beat, alt open: "I wanted so badly for it to be one thing."
- Alt sequencing: could go directly after "just me" (before the dreams) to group with the
  resignation — but loses the flare-and-collapse tension of bracketing it between the two
  "it's me" beats (dreams, then genetic).
- Ties to the "one thing"/silver-bullet thread (Ch02:5,11,27,33,61; Ch03:31; Ch09:19;
  Ch10:7; Ch12:3) and its deliberate inversion at Ch25:64. Candidate for a wiki motif page.

## Concept 6 — Nowhere to turn / the system had no answer (Ch01 + Ch02, SPLIT)

One idea split across two chapters to keep each chapter's register intact: the emotional
half (no one understood; couldn't articulate it) goes to Ch01; the institutional half (the
doctor route came up short) opens Ch02. Both kept strictly first person.

### 6a — Isolation half (Ch01)

Tightened to drop "I didn't really know where to turn" — that idea is now delivered
concretely by 6b, so Ch01 carries only what's unique to it (no one understood; couldn't
describe it). Avoids overlap with the Ch02 opener.

**Insertion point:** after current Ch01 line ending "…got me through… until they didn't."
(the afternoon-dip/sugar-caffeine paragraph), before "After years of trying and failing, I
started to wonder whether the problem was just me."

**Insert:**

> Unless you've lived it, it's hard to understand. I'd smile to myself when someone said,
> "God, I'm wrecked today — the fire alarm went off last night and it took me two hours to
> get back to sleep." That was their worst night in memory. It was better than most of
> mine. Part of the problem was that I couldn't even describe what was wrong. My thoughts
> were so scattered that "I can't sleep" was about as far as I could get.

**Notes:**
- Fire-alarm anecdote is the sharpest illustration of the "if you haven't suffered you
  don't know" gulf; the light "I'd smile to myself" gives the chapter a rare, welcome
  lightness. Cut that one sentence if a leaner passage is wanted — paragraph still stands.

### 6b — Doctor / system half (Ch02) — opens the chapter

Replaces the current cold-open "I kept looking." as the first beat; "I kept looking" is
retained immediately after, now with a concrete antecedent (the doctor route failed, so he
kept going). Built from the author's actual experience: GP prescribed once but wouldn't
repeat it, pointed him toward root-cause help (a psychologist), and said a sleep study
likely wouldn't help as it didn't sound like apnea. Doctor reads as conscientious, not
incompetent — the limit is framed as role/scope, never stated as "not their fault".

**Insertion point:** opening of Ch02, before the existing "I kept looking." (current line
3). New order: [doctor beat] → "I kept looking." → "Books, podcasts, YouTube, forums…".

**Insert (becomes the chapter's first prose):**

> I started with my doctor. He gave me something to help me sleep, but made it clear he
> didn't want to prescribe it again. What I really needed, he said, was to get to the root
> of it — and for that I'd have to see a psychologist, or someone like one. He didn't think
> a sleep study was worth it either. It didn't sound like apnea, and that's mostly what it
> would be looking for.
>
> He was right on every count. It was also where the trail went cold.
>
> A GP is a generalist, and this wasn't a generalist's kind of problem. The tools he had
> were a prescription he'd rather not repeat and a referral onward — and that kind of help
> is hard to reach, with few practitioners and long waits.

**Notes:**
- Register: first person throughout (fixed from an earlier impersonal advice-column
  draft). The sleep-study limitation now emerges from the doctor's own words, so no generic
  "you" explainer is needed.
- Doctor-protective framing carried by structure, not disclaimer: "a generalist / not a
  generalist's kind of problem" (scope, not competence); "a prescription he'd rather not
  repeat" (conscientious); "He was right on every count" (credits him). Deliberately avoids
  any "it's not their fault"-type line, which would say the quiet part aloud.
- Punch: "He was right on every count. It was also where the trail went cold." kept as its
  own hard-stop beat; the generalist explanation follows so it doesn't dilute the punch.
- Fact-check (web, Jul 2026): claims are defensible. Sleep studies are primarily for
  sleep-disordered breathing, not insomnia causation; guideline first-line for chronic
  insomnia is CBT-I with medication discouraged (sedative-hypnotic side effects real);
  trained CBT-I/behavioural-sleep providers genuinely scarce with long waits. Evidence is
  mostly US; direction holds in AU. No hard numbers in prose, so no numeric-locale risk.
- Honesty caveat on final line: "few practitioners and long waits" is a general (supported)
  statement about accessing specialised help, not necessarily the author's lived wait. If
  the psychologist route was never pursued, optional trim: "and that kind of help isn't
  easy to get to."
- Optional pivot polish (not applied): Ch02 line 5 could open "So I turned to books,
  podcasts…" to make the hand-off explicit; reads fine unchanged.
