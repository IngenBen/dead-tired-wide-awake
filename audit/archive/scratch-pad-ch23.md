## STAGE 1 — Beat analysis

Main message: eating too close to bed runs the activating system (digestion, heat,
insulin) at exactly the hour the body needs to cool and calm, so late food costs sleep
quality even when it doesn't cost sleep onset. Fix: finish the last substantial meal ~3h
before bed; if genuinely hungry, a small snack beats lying awake; size matters more than
composition.

Beats that are working are not listed. Only problem beats below.

### Beat problem 1 — the mechanism is stated twice (para 5 → para 6)

Para 5 already lands the mechanism: eating shifts the body into an active state, heat
rises. Para 6 then re-states the same point ("Digestion is the activating system switched
on, when it should be stand down") before adding its actual new content — that the problem
is *timing*. The recap blunts para 6's real job. Tighten para 6 so it leads with timing
and doesn't re-explain digestion.

Context (para 5 unchanged, para 6 amended, para 7 unchanged):

When you eat, your body shifts into an active state — exactly what it needs to do to
digest the meal. Metabolism rises, core temperature follows, insulin is released, and your
gut gets to work.

The problem is simply one of timing. **It's the** ~~These are the~~ same conditions that
prevent sleep from starting properly**, arriving at the worst possible hour.** ~~You're
asking the body to cool down while it's generating heat. Digestion is the activating system
switched on, when it should be stand down.~~ **You're asking the body to cool down while
it's generating heat — the activating system running when it should be standing down.**

The result isn't always that you can't fall asleep. Sometimes you can if the food-coma
feeling is strong...

*Note:* this keeps the heat/cool image (the chapter's strongest line) but folds the
redundant "digestion is the activating system" sentence into it rather than stating it as
a fresh point.

### 2. Cadence and flow

anything, I thought the opposite") is a natural landing point before the food-coma
confession opens. Recommend a paragraph break, no words changed:

Evenings are when life happens. Sometimes a race to get dinner on time, kids through

For a long time I didn't think any of it was relevant to what happened an hour later when I
closed my eyes. If anything, I thought the opposite.

**[paragraph break]**

You know that warm, heavy feeling that sometimes follows a big meal — what some people call
a food coma? I loved that feeling. It felt like the body making a case for sleep. I thought
I could use it.

*Check:* the break introduces no repetition and the following line ("It's a bit like
alcohol though…") still follows cleanly from the food-coma confession.
