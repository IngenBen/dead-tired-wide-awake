## 1. Factual slip (checked against chapter 16)

Paragraph: "The habits were actually doing three things..."

Before: Each one seemed almost too small to matter: light exposure in the morning, a three-hour gap before dinner, a consistent wake time.

After: Each one seemed almost too small to matter: light exposure in the morning, a three-hour gap between dinner and bed, a consistent wake time.

## 2. Filler word

Before: The habits were actually doing three things: training your body clock, building your calming system, and removing what kept you activated at night, so sleep could come when it should.

After: The habits were doing three things: training your body clock, building your calming system, and removing what kept you activated at night, so sleep could come when it should.

## 3. Saying that sounds deep but is unclear

Paragraph: "The progress is gradual..."

Before: The progress is gradual, but the moment you recognise it doesn't have to be.

After: The progress is gradual, but recognising it can happen in a moment.

## 4. Redundant pair

Paragraph: "There's no single morning where the problem is suddenly "fixed.""

Before: And then, at some point, it becomes undeniable: the change you'd been waiting for is unmistakably real.

After: And then, at some point, it becomes undeniable: the change you'd been waiting for is real.

## 5. Firm generalisation

## 6. Repeated word, and trailing space

Before: there's a free app to track your progress, and the **Sleep Builders** community to ask questions and share progress.

After: there's a free app to track your progress, and the **Sleep Builders** community to ask questions and share what's working.

Before: The mind that wouldn't switch off finally can. (trailing space after the full stop)

After: Remove the trailing space. No wording change.

## Left alone on purpose

- The paragraph about the author's wife. Specific and lived; unquoted dialogue after the colons is deliberate.
- "Nobody tries to get fit on the night of a race." Good analogy.
- "Most nights is enough."
- "You don't need to do everything. You need to do something. Then the next thing." Author rhythm.
- "None of it felt like fixing sleep. That was the point." Restates, but a callback; kept.
- The closing lines ("You're the CEO of Sleep Incorporated... Now go get some sleep."). Fitting ending in the author's voice.
- "You are not trying to fix sleep directly. You are creating the conditions..." Corrects a belief the reader holds.
