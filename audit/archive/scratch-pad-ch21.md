## STAGE 2 — Six-category review

### 1. Contradictions

**Contradiction 1 — "amount matters less" (para 35) vs "step three is reduction" (para 37).**
Para 35 tells the reader the content matters more than the time: "The difference matters more than the amount of time you spend on the phone." Two paragraphs later, step three is *reduce the amount of time*. It isn't a hard contradiction — content first, volume second is a defensible order — but a reader who just absorbed "amount matters less" can trip on being told to cut the amount. Fix turns the tension into explicit sequencing: both matter, do step two now, let step three follow once the feed has settled.

Context (before → fix → after):

Then start clicking toward things that genuinely interest you instead... Once the feed changes, the pull toward spiking content weakens on its own.

The third step is reduction. **Content matters more than volume — but you really want both. So start with step two today, the moment you finish this chapter; let step three wait until the new feed has settled and the pull has eased.** ~~Do it less — through the day.~~ **Then cut the time down too.** Your evenings are already handled: after Chapter 17, the phone is away once the wind-down begins. So this step is about the daytime scrolling — the spare minutes, the queues, the gaps between things. ~~Once the first two steps are in place, this one~~ **By then it** gets easier than you'd expect — as long as you hold strong and keep resisting the urge to click the old content. The moment you start engaging with it again, the feed notices.

*Check:* resolves the tension by sequencing rather than denying it; also removes the doubled "through the day" and the third restatement of the step-two-before-three order ("By then it gets easier" replaces "Once the first two steps are in place"). No new seam.

**Contradiction 2 — blue light downplayed here (para 7) vs quantified as significant in the ch15/ch17 footnote.** *(cross-chapter — light-and-sleep: ch06, ch12, ch15, ch16, ch21)*
Para 7 says blue-light blockers gave "only modest, inconsistent benefits" and frames light as a minor factor. But the footnote carried in ch15 (and referenced in ch17) quantifies a phone at 30 cm at 25–50 lux — above the 17-lux threshold it names for melatonin suppression — which is the stated reason a TV is preferable to a phone. So the footnote treats phone light as a real suppressant while this chapter waves blue light away wholesale. Fix reframes the whole paragraph on the author's intended arc: the "villain" headline was wrong; it's light *and* content; content is the worse of the two; so dimming the screen can't get you out of it. That keeps the chapter's thesis while acknowledging the light as real (consistent with the footnote).

Full paragraph rewrite (before → after):

~~"Blue light is the villain" was the early headline, and it had its merits — screen light can suppress melatonin and shift the body clock. But more recent findings show what you do on the screen matters more than the light the screen emits: stimulating, emotionally engaging content delays sleep onset more than light exposure alone. Studies with blue light blockers produced only modest, inconsistent benefits. The light may play a role. But the content is the bigger problem.~~

**"Blue light is the villain" was the early headline — and it wasn't quite right. The light is real: it can suppress melatonin and nudge the body clock later. But it's not the whole story. It's the light and what's on the screen — and the content is the worse of the two. More recent work pins more of the delay on stimulating, emotional material than on the light itself. So dimming your screen only takes care of a sliver of the problem. You can't filter your way out of it.**

*Check / knock-ons:* resolves the cross-chapter contradiction (light now acknowledged as real). Also **subsumes Cadence 1** — the dense clinical sentence is gone, replaced by the chapter's normal short-sentence rhythm; treat Cadence 1 as handled by this rewrite. **Weak point 1 still stands**: "More recent work pins more of the delay…" remains an uncited science claim and needs a reference before the social/app phase.
