## STAGE 1 — Beat analysis

**Main messages of the chapter.** (1) The evening doesn't need to be elaborate — its real
job is just to *stop interfering* with a wind-down the body is already starting. (2) Build a
short, repeatable end-of-day sequence inside whatever window you actually have. (3) Set a
consistent bedtime by working back from a fixed wake time — flexible within reason, but the
*morning* is the anchor that never moves.

The spine is sound and the beats are mostly clean. Reported below are only the beats that
have problems. Beats not listed are working and are left alone.

### 1. Contradictions

**Contradiction 1 (L19) — "no devices" immediately followed by "I watch TV."**
> From there, no devices. I watch TV for at least 30 minutes...

On its face these conflict — a TV is a device. The intended meaning is "no *interactive/handheld*
devices (phone, tablet, laptop)," with passive TV allowed (consistent with the ch15 blue-light
footnote: TV at distance ≈ 5 lux, phone ≈ 25–50 lux). The logic is sound but the wording reads
as self-contradiction. Fix — name what's actually banned:

Previous (L17, unchanged):

> By the time the kids are down, the lights are already down. Homework and reading happens at desk lamps — lower, directional light that isn't flooding the room. It's not a deliberate sleep strategy at that point, it's just practical. But it means the handover to the Sleep Executives has already quietly begun before the evening is technically over.

Amended (L19):

> From there, ~~no devices.~~ **no phones — nothing in my hand, nothing I can scroll.** I watch TV for at least 30 minutes — something undemanding, nothing that's going to spike the Wake Executives. A sitcom, a mild comedy. You can afford something a little more activating than middle of the night, but avoid things like intense dramas or thrillers.

Following (L21, unchanged):

> When the TV goes off, the evening is over. Teeth, PJs, bed...

No other internal or cross-chapter contradictions found. The evening TV calibration ("a little
more activating than middle of the night") is *consistent* with ch15's stricter can't-sleep
standard, and the morning-anchor / flexible-bedtime stance is consistent with ch16 and ch06.

### 3. Weak points

**Weak point 1 (L31) — unsupported science claim.**
> The science says keep your bedtime within 30 minutes of the same time, each night. There is reasonable evidence behind this — even modest deviations later than your usual [bedtime] show measurable effects on heart rate and recovery the following day, and the effect compounds the further you stretch it.

This is a `status: established-science` assertion ("the science says," "reasonable evidence,"
"measurable effects on heart rate and recovery") with nothing behind it — exactly the kind of
claim CLAUDE.md flags as needing support before the social/app phase. The "heart rate and
recovery" framing reads as tracker-derived (WHOOP/Oura, per the ch10/ch02 sleep-tracker thread);
if so, grounding it in the author's own tracker data would both support it and fit the
narrator-as-patient-zero voice better than an appeal to "the science." **Action: add to the
unsupported-science list for the citation pass; consider regrounding in personal tracker data.**

**Weak point 2 (L23) — "Lying down means one thing" is cryptic.** Addressed in Cadence 2; noted
here because the vagueness is also an argumentative weak spot, not only a rhythm one.

**Personal grounding (style-standard check):** the chapter clears the minimum bar — the "three
kids to bed, then a short window" shape (L11) is specific and load-bearing, and the
bed-a-bit-earlier catch-up (L35) is a named personal practice. It's grounding-by-presence rather
than one vivid scene; adequate, not a finding, but the chapter is lighter on a single memorable
moment than the ch16 walk or ch15 binge-watching anecdote.

### 5. Repetition

**Repetition 1 (L19) — evening TV criteria duplicate ch15's can't-sleep criteria. (Strongest.)**
ch15 spends several paragraphs on content selection: "mildly amusing — nothing with suspense,
nothing with a plot I needed to concentrate on... Definitely no suspense or thrillers." ch17 L19
repeats the same rule set: "something undemanding... A sitcom, a mild comedy... avoid things like
intense dramas or thrillers." ch17 *does* add a genuine new calibration ("a little more
activating than middle of the night"), which is the part worth keeping. Recommend ch17 lean on
the ch15 standard rather than re-listing it — keep the calibration, shed the re-stated criteria.
(index: not yet cross-linked; `cant-sleep-routine` ch15/ch18 and `evening-routine` ch17 should
reference each other.)

**Repetition 2 (L23) — "bed is for sleep" now appears in 3 chapters.** ch12 ("The bed is for
sleep; remove anything that tells your brain otherwise"), ch15 (the whole bed-equals-sleep
association argument), ch17 L23. Per the CLAUDE.md 3+-chapter duplication rule this is a flag.
ch17's *angle* is fresh (don't carry unresolved problems/conversations into bed), so keep that;
but the bare "The bed is for sleep" assertion is the canonical ch15 line. Recommend ch17 keep its
unique angle and not re-assert the principle from scratch. (index `bed-sleep-association`:
ch12, ch15, ch17 — correctly already lists all three.)

**Repetition 3 (L39/L43) — morning-anchor re-states ch16.** ch16 is entirely about "wake time
does not move / you are setting a clock." ch17 L39 ("The one thing that doesn't flex is the
morning. The wake-up time is the anchor...") restates the thesis. Some restatement is justified —
ch17 needs to position the bedtime *relative* to that anchor — so L39 as a pointer is fine; the
extra "Protecting that morning signal is what keeps the whole rhythm stable" is the re-argued
part. Light trim, not a cut.

**Repetition 4 (within-chapter) — "window" carries three different meanings.** The term does
heavy, shifting duty: the *sleep window* (the canonical convergence concept, L5), the *leftover
evening time* (L11, L15), and the *±30-minute bedtime tolerance* (L31, L37→now folded). Reusing
the book's load-bearing term ("sleep window," per index, ch06/07/08/17/21) for two mundane senses
dilutes it and can momentarily confuse. Recommend reserving "window" for the sleep window and
renaming the others (e.g. "the time that's left," "the 30-minute tolerance").

**Repetition 5 (motif, low priority) — "exhausted but can't sleep" (L9).** Echoes the recurring
"tired but wired / exhausted but wide awake" motif (ch06, ch07, ch16, ch21). This reads as the
author's deliberate emphasis motif (style notes: repetition for emphasis), so leave it.
