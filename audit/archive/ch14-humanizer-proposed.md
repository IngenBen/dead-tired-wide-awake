## 7. Soften firm claims (added by author)

Wording follows what the claims knowledge base and the Notes section support.

Paragraph: opening of Phase 2 ("Phase 1 was about getting set up...")

Before: And caffeine is first, not because it's the easiest, but because for a significant number of people it is the single habit most likely to be disrupting their sleep.

After: And caffeine is first, not because it's the easiest, but because for anyone who drinks it in the afternoon it is likely the habit doing the most damage to their sleep.

Paragraph: "When the caffeine wears off and the adenosine that's been building behind it floods back..."

Before: It exists for everyone, but caffeine makes it significantly worse, turning a manageable dip into a wall.

After: It exists for everyone, but caffeine makes it worse over time, turning a manageable dip into a wall.

Paragraph: "There's also the morning."

Before: Both point to the same recommendation: delay the first coffee by sixty to ninety minutes.

After: Both suggest the same thing: delay the first coffee by sixty to ninety minutes.

Paragraph: "This is the one I'm still working on."

Before: Everything I've read backs it, and that's part of what keeps me at it.

After: The evidence is thinner than I'd like, but the reasoning makes sense to me, and that's part of what keeps me at it.

Note: the Notes entry for this claim calls it "a reasonable, low-confidence practical tip rather than established science" and says no tracker-confirmed difference has been shown, so the softer wording matches it.

## Left alone on purpose

- "It wasn't a willpower problem... That's not a character flaw. That's biology.": corrects a belief the author held and is central to the book.
- "caffeine is first, not because it's the easiest, but because...": answers a real expectation about the order.
- "I'm still too scared to do the maths on my seven-cup days.": dry humour, in the author's voice.
- The loop fragments ("Tired in the afternoon. Another coffee. Wired at night..."): deliberate rhythm.
- "The morning will come.": wry and effective.
- "Sleep deprivation measurably reduces your capacity for self-control": left as written; the claims knowledge base has a matching sourced entry (Pilcher et al. 2015).
