## Stage 1 — Beat analysis — [2026-06-16]

### Main messages of the chapter

1. Sugar was an energy-management crutch for the every-couple-of-hours slump; quitting by willpower never stuck.
2. What actually reduced the sugar wasn't tackling sugar head-on — it was fixing sleep first, which took the pressure off the craving and made deliberate cutting-down possible.

### Problem beats

#### Beat A — "What actually resolved it … it just happened" (para at L11)

**Issue.** This beat overclaims. It says the cravings "reduced on their own," that "I didn't engineer that — it just happened, as a downstream effect of the rest of the program clicking into place." But the chapter's own closing reflection (L53) says the opposite: the sleep "took enough pressure off the craving to make cutting down manageable. And cutting down helped the sleep improve further." There the reduction is a *deliberate* act that sleep made possible. So the chapter opens by saying the program did it *for* him, and closes by saying he still had to do it — with the program giving him a head start. The two ends contradict. The opening also lands the same meta-point as the close, so as written it both overclaims and pre-empts its own payoff.

**Fix.** Shift from passive ("wasn't anything I did") to a *focus* framing, demote "it just happened" to "the program did most of the heavy lifting; a conscious effort did the rest," and (separately agreed this session) expand the mechanism with the willpower + leptin/ghrelin reward science so the claim is supported rather than asserted. Marked-up core change:

Context before (unchanged):

> But the craving for the energy it gave me didn't go away. I was still hitting the wall every couple of hours. I'd just stopped reaching for the same thing to get over it.

Amended:

> What ~~actually resolved it, in the end, wasn't anything I did about sugar directly. It was everything else.~~ **did most of the heavy lifting, in the end, wasn't focusing on sugar directly. It was focusing on everything else.**
>
> *(then the agreed willpower + leptin/ghrelin + reward-system expansion, ending:)* **The cravings lost enough of their grip to become resistible. I didn't engineer that part directly — the rest of the program clicking into place took the pressure off. A conscious effort did the rest.**

Context after (unchanged):

> That's why sugar comes this late in the program. Caffeine and sugar both put pressure on the same systems — tackling them simultaneously is too much change at once.

> Cross-ref: full expanded passage held in chat this session; to be filed as the ch24 proposal.

#### Beat B — the 2–3am wake (para at L29)

**Issue, part 1 (contradiction).** The beat says "**That's what's behind it** — a blood sugar dip triggering a cortisol surge." That asserts the sugar dip as *the* cause of the 2–3am wake. Ch07 (Cortisol, L47) already owns this exact phenomenon and gives a different cause: as melatonin eases from ~2am, any cortisol "riding higher than it should" jumps in to fill the gap — driven by busy mind, caffeine, screens, sleep-debt stress. Two chapters, two sole causes for the same event. Reader who absorbed ch07 will feel the overwrite.

**Issue, part 2 (terminology).** The beat calls this 2–3am slot a "window." Across the book "window" is a reserved term of art for the **sleep-onset window** (defined ch06; reused ch08/ch12/ch16/ch17, and ch07 uses "missed my window" the same way). Using it here for the time you're pulled *out* of sleep collides with that canonical meaning.

**Fix.** Demote sugar from *the* cause to *one more trigger* of the same surge ch07 described, add the callback, and drop "window." Marked-up:

Context before (unchanged):

> The result isn't always that you can't fall asleep. Sometimes you can. But the sleep you get is shallower, more fragmented, easier to break.

Amended:

> And for a lot of people, there's a specific ~~window~~ **hour** — somewhere between two and three in the morning — where you wake up alert, anxious, or hot, for no obvious reason. ~~That's what's behind it — a blood sugar dip triggering a cortisol surge in the middle of the night, pulling you out of deep sleep at the worst possible time.~~ **The cortisol chapter explained why it tends to be then: as melatonin eases in the small hours, any cortisol riding higher than it should jumps in to fill the gap. A late sugar hit is one more thing that can light that fuse — a blood sugar dip provoking a cortisol surge of its own, pulling you out of deep sleep at the worst possible time.** It's easy to assume you woke to use the bathroom — but often the cortisol surge comes first, and the bathroom is simply what you notice once you're awake.

Context after (unchanged):

> Knowing that changes what you do about it.

> Cross-ref: ch07 L47. Recommend a `window` wiki page recording it as a canonical term (sleep-onset only).

#### Beat C — opening meta-point (L11) vs closing meta-point (L53) — bookend overlap

**Issue.** Both beats make the same higher-order claim: "I didn't fix sugar by tackling sugar; everything else did." Opening states it; close restates it as the upward-spiral reflection. Keep the close focused on the *reciprocal spiral*; keep the open focused on the *obstacle being lifted*. Flag only — no cut proposed.
