## Full paragraph after merge

Caffeine raises cortisol, and more so in people who rarely drink it than in daily drinkers. In people who had stopped caffeine for several days, doses through the day kept cortisol raised into the evening. In daily drinkers the response was smaller and shorter, though an early-afternoon dose still raised it for some hours. The book proposes that cortisol settles more slowly in people who sleep poorly, so they respond more like infrequent drinkers than daily ones.
