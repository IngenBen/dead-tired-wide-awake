## Find

Magnesium L-threonate is often recommended on the basis that it crosses the blood–brain barrier more readily than other forms. That advantage rests primarily on animal studies; human sleep RCT evidence for L-threonate specifically is very limited. Magnesium is widely recommended for sleep, but few high-quality studies support it, and the overall evidence that any form meaningfully improves sleep is low quality. The most direct human evidence on how it might work comes from a small trial in older adults with insomnia, where magnesium improved sleep measures and lowered cortisol; this may reflect correcting a shortfall common at that age rather than a benefit for everyone.
