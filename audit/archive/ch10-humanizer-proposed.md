# Chapter 10 humanizer pass: proposed edits

Source: `/book/ch10 - Taking Ownership.md` (version last modified 2026-09-25). Proposals only; apply by hand.
Skipped by author: original items 2 and 5. Item 10 (soften the strong claim) added at the author's request.
Items keep their original numbers so they match the chat review.

## 1. Double colon and run-on

Paragraph: "You met those two settings back in Chapter 4..."

Before: You met those two settings back in Chapter 4: they're built to take turns, and bad sleep almost always comes down to one of two things: a calming system too weak to take over, or the two swapping on the wrong schedule.

After: You met those two settings back in Chapter 4. They're built to take turns, and bad sleep almost always comes down to one of two things: a calming system too weak to take over, or the two swapping on the wrong schedule.

## 3. Staged candor and a stock reassurance

Paragraph: "A fair question before you start is: how long?"

Before: A fair question before you start is: how long? The honest answer is it depends on where you're starting and how quickly you move. ... Go slower and that's fine too. This isn't a race.

After: A fair question before you start is: how long? It depends on where you're starting and how quickly you move. ... Go slower and that's fine too.

## 4. Small correction to the "not the end" contrast

Paragraph: "But the moment worth waiting for..."

Before: But the moment worth waiting for isn't the end. It's the first time the change becomes undeniable...

After: But the moment worth waiting for comes before the end. It's the first time the change becomes undeniable...

## 6. Grammar and stacked intensifiers in the app paragraph

Paragraph: "I strongly recommend recording whatever you're working on..."

Before: It's free, so I'd really encourage you to use it. It'll help you navigate the program, track where you're at, and prompt those check-ins. If you're like me, I didn't always do everything, every day. I needed reminding and reinforcement.

After: It's free, so I'd encourage you to use it. It'll help you navigate the program, track where you're at, and prompt those check-ins. If you're like me, you won't always do everything, every day. I needed reminding and reinforcement.

## 7. Contrast and filler in the paper paragraph

Paragraph: "If an app isn't for you, paper works just as well."

Before: What matters isn't how. It's that you do it. Writing things down is a simple and powerful way to help with commitment.

After: What matters is that you do it. Writing things down helps with commitment.

## 8. Staged "here's the thing", repeated "other people", and "url"

Paragraph: "Either way, app or paper..."

Before: Either way, app or paper, here's the thing I'd encourage most of all. People are far more likely to see the program to the end if they are doing it with other people, working the same habits, answering questions and helping each other through challenges. It really is worth joining the community. It's called **Sleep Builders**, and you'll find it at the same url, www.sleepbuilders.com.au.

After: Either way, app or paper, what I'd encourage most is joining others doing the program. People are far more likely to see it through if they are working the same habits together, answering questions and helping each other through challenges. It's worth joining the community. It's called **Sleep Builders**, and you'll find it at the same URL, www.sleepbuilders.com.au.

## 9. Small grammar fixes

Paragraph: "Massage, for example, didn't make the cut."

Before: But the cost and time away from the kids' dinner and bed routine, just wasn't realistic for me.

After: But the cost and time away from the kids' dinner and bed routine just wasn't realistic for me.

Paragraph: "Your schedule and family life won't be exactly the same as mine."

Before: ...give you an understanding why, and encourage you to be imperfect...

After: ...help you understand why, and encourage you to be imperfect...

## 10. Soften the strong claim (added by author)

Paragraph: "Each habit is based on solid, well-established sleep research..."

Before: Each habit is based on solid, well-established sleep research, the same science we walked through in the last four chapters.

After: Each habit draws on sleep research, the same science we walked through in the last four chapters. Where the evidence is thinner or still debated, the Notes at the back say so.

Note: the Notes section (`/book/endnotes.md`) is titled "Notes" and concedes weak or contested evidence in places, so the added sentence matches what the reader will find there.

## Left alone on purpose

- "I wasn't the problem. But I was the solution.": corrects a belief the author held and sets up the chapter.
- "But that's not a program. That's a list.": gives a reason.
- The massage example: specific, reads as the author's.
- The three-part question ("what does science say is ideal, what's realistically achievable...", "do I still get results..."): three distinct parts.
- Original items 2 (the "Slow changes that stick beat fast ones that don't." closer) and 5 ("One last thing before Phase 1 begins."): skipped by author.

## Flags for the author (not proposed as edits)

- Overlap with chapter 2: the "undeniable change breaks the old story, then motivation takes care of itself" passage repeats chapter 2's version almost word for word. Duplication candidate for the full audit.
- Three signals: chapter 4 names them "activate, calm, timing". This chapter's three jobs are "train the body clock, build the calming system, stop activation at the wrong times". They map loosely; consider matching the wording.
