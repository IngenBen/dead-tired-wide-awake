# Ch10 thesis proposals: Taking Ownership

Ch10 opens Part 3 and already names 'a training program' (L9) and the three jobs as training (L15). Two things work against the thesis. The opening line, 'I was the problem', comes straight after 'Not a flaw in you. Not a bad sleeper' (Ch09 L82) and echoes the old belief from Ch01 L25 ('whether the problem was just me'). And 'a fault in the handover' is repair wording (Decision 2). Two edits.

## Summary of changes

✅ **10-A ('I wasn't the problem' version) and 10-B (as written) APPROVED by the author 2026-09-24.**

- **10-A** (L5): **'I was the problem' becomes 'I wasn't the problem. But I was the solution.'** Ownership stays (the solution was his to build), but the line no longer says the problem was *him*, which is the belief the book takes apart. 'Mine to fix' (fix as the goal) becomes 'mine to turn around', which sets up the Ch26 close ('who will turn the company around'). **An author-voice call, see the alternative.**
- **10-B** (L13): 'bad sleep is almost always a fault in the handover' becomes 'comes down to one of two things… Both can be trained.' It removes the repair word (Decision 2) and hands straight to L15's 'Train the body clock. Build the calming system.'

**No change, and why:**

- **L7** ('something outside myself to fix it… the one thing I was missing'): the wrong approach (Decision 3, keep).
- **L9** ('I got to work on a training program'): already the thesis. Ch09 L84 (9-B) now sets it up and Ch26 L35 (26-E) pays it off.
- **L15** (the three jobs), **L17** ('each building on the last'), **L23** ('That's not a program. That's a list.'), **L29** ('a habit you sustain beats a perfect protocol'), **L33** ('Slow changes that stick'), **L35** ('this isn't a race'): all already the thesis in plain words.
- **L37** ('I'll never be a good sleeper'): states and rejects the belief (Decision 6, keep).
- **L41–45** (tracking, community): a training log and training partners in all but name. No fitness wording needed (Decision 5).

## 10-A · L5

**Why:** The book's key message is that bad sleep isn't who you are. 'I was the problem' says the opposite, one page after 'Not a flaw in you. Not a bad sleeper.' The flip keeps the ownership point and makes it sharper: the problem wasn't him, the solution was. 'Mine to fix' is fix as the goal (Decision 3). 'Turn around' is plain, fits the company metaphor and sets up Ch26 L35.  
**Decision ref:** Thread check ('just a bad sleeper' → trainable); Decision 3

*[L1]* # PART 3 – The Program

*[L3]* # Chapter 10 - Taking Ownership

▶ *[L5]* I ~~was~~ **wasn't** the problem~~, and~~**. But** I was the solution. I was the CEO of Sleep Incorporated, which meant it was mine to ~~fix~~ **turn around**.

*[L7]* That felt heavy but understanding it was actually a relief. I'd spent years looking for something outside myself to fix it. A pill. A routine I'd read about somewhere. A YouTube expert with the one thing I was missing. None of it stuck, because none of it had addressed the whole company.

*[L9]* Once I stopped waiting to be rescued and started running the company myself, I got to work on a training program.

**Clean:**

> I wasn't the problem. But I was the solution. I was the CEO of Sleep Incorporated, which meant it was mine to turn around.

**Check L7 after the change:** 'That felt heavy' still works. Being the solution is the heavy part, and 'a relief' is that it was in his hands.

**Alternative (keeps the author's opening):** 'I was the problem, and I was the solution. I was the CEO of Sleep Incorporated, which meant it was mine to turn around.' This only changes 'fix'. It keeps the ownership punch, but leaves 'I was the problem' sitting against Ch09 L82 and Ch01 L25.

## 10-B · L13

**Why:** 'A fault in the handover' is repair wording (Decision 2). The two causes stay exactly as written. 'Both can be trained' names the thesis and leads into L15 ('Train the body clock. Build the calming system.'). The em dash goes: a colon here introduces a genuine two-item list.  
**Decision ref:** Decision 2

*[L11]* For most of my life I'd been stuck on the activated setting — wound up, switched on, a system that never clocked off. What was missing was the other one: a calming system strong enough to take over at night.

▶ *[L13]* You met those two settings back in Chapter 4: they're built to take turns, and bad sleep ~~is~~ almost always ~~a fault in the handover —~~ **comes down to one of two things:** a calming system too weak to take over, or the two swapping on the wrong schedule. **Both can be trained.**

*[L15]* So the habits do three jobs, the three signals from Chapter 4 turned into daily action. Train the body clock. Build the calming system. Stop activation at the wrong times. Every habit in the chapters ahead is one of those three. Know each habit's job and you'll see how it works toward a good night's sleep.

**Clean:**

> You met those two settings back in Chapter 4: they're built to take turns, and bad sleep almost always comes down to one of two things: a calming system too weak to take over, or the two swapping on the wrong schedule. Both can be trained.

**Note:** that makes two colons in one sentence. If it reads heavy, the first can go: 'You met those two settings back in Chapter 4. They're built to take turns, and bad sleep almost always comes down to one of two things: …'
