# Ch07 thesis proposals: Cortisol

Ch07 already has the book's clearest training image in plain words: *spike and return* (L19). It's the same idea as heart-rate recovery, so it needs no analogy added. The job is vocabulary. 'Well-regulated' (Decision 2) and 'a good sleeper' (Decision 6) change, and one sentence that makes cortisol the whole program (a one-thing risk) is softened. Three edits.

## Summary of changes

✅ **7-A to 7-C APPROVED by the author 2026-09-24.**

- **7-A** (L19): 'a well-regulated system' becomes 'a well-trained system' (Decision 2). Em dash as in the em dash pass.
- **7-B** (L43): 'Everything in the program is… about getting cortisol to stand down on time' becomes 'Much of the program'. This keeps the point but stops cortisol reading as the one thing, which also sits better with Ch10 L15 (three jobs). Em dash becomes a full stop. - **L7, L37** ('Getting fitter'): literal fitness, as content of the racing mind. The framing rule keeps the analogy away from it.

## 7-B · L43

**Decision ref:** Thesis (no one thing)

*[L41]* Cortisol and melatonin should work in opposition — in the evening as cortisol falls, melatonin rises. When that handover happens cleanly, sleep follows.

▶ *[L43]* This is activating and calming at their clearest. Cortisol is the activating chemistry~~ — while~~**. While** it stays high, calming can't take over for the night. ~~Everything in~~ **Much of** the program is, one way or another, about getting cortisol to stand down on time.

*[L45]* This is the mind that won't switch off at night. Not a flaw in you — cortisol, still high when it should be falling. Bring it down on time, and the mind comes down with it.

**Clean:**

> This is activating and calming at their clearest. Cortisol is the activating chemistry. While it stays high, calming can't take over for the night. Much of the program is, one way or another, about getting cortisol to stand down on time.
