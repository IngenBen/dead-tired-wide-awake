## Finding 5 — Two separated "recognised therapy" callouts

Consolidate ACT-i (L33) and CBT-i (L57) into one beat, differentiating their roles.

Edit **line 33**:
Getting up does something lying there can't: it removes the pressure to sleep. In bed, not sleeping, you are — consciously or not — struggling. ~~If you've come across ACT-i — Acceptance and Commitment Therapy for insomnia — acceptance of wakefulness is one of its core principles, for exactly this reason: the struggle generates more arousal than the wakefulness itself.~~ **Two established therapies land on the same instruction. CBT-i — cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia — makes "don't lie in bed awake; get up" a central recommendation. ACT-i, its acceptance-based cousin, adds the why: acceptance of wakefulness, because the struggle generates more arousal than the wakefulness itself. (I only worked out the match after learning it the hard way.)** The routine is a practical version of that idea. When I can't sleep, I do this. A protocol instead of a struggle. In the language of the program, you're refusing to feed the activating side — no frustration, no dopamine — so the calming side can come back on its own.

Remove the now-duplicate CBT-i sentence from **line 57**:
…The change accumulates over weeks, not in a single night. ~~If you've come across CBT-i — cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia — you'll recognise this routine; not lying in bed awake is one of its central recommendations, which I only found out after learning the hard way.~~

The first few nights may feel worse, not better; the new pattern hasn't had enough repetitions yet. That's expected. So the next time you're lying awake at 2am, you'll have something to do — and even when it doesn't get you straight back to sleep, it's moving you in the right direction, one night at a time.**
