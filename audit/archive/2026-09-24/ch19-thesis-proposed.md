## 19-C · L55

**Why:** 'Balance' is a third diagnosis word. Decision 2 replaces it with training framing: a calming system strong enough to take its turn. 'Take its turn' picks up L53.  
**Decision ref:** Decision 2

*[L53]* Most people with persistent sleep problems spend too much time in the activated state — stuck in go-mode, always on alert. The calming system rarely gets a turn, so it weakens from lack of use until it can't bring them back. The two are supposed to take turns — one speaks, the other listens, then they swap. Instead, the activating system has grown so loud it never stops talking. And the calming system has forgotten how to speak up for itself.

▶ *[L55]* Meditation, done consistently, builds that muscle. By gradually strengthening the calming system, you are teaching it to hold its ground~~ —~~**,** and the activating system to back off. Once ~~that balance begins to return~~ **it's strong enough to take its turn again**, you will have it when you need it~~ —~~**,** and reaching for it in the middle of the night will start to work.

*[L57]* This might sound a bit abstract, but the calming system has an anatomy. A physical pathway it actually runs through.

*[L59]* Its primary channel is the vagus nerve. […]

**Clean:**

> Meditation, done consistently, builds that muscle. By gradually strengthening the calming system, you are teaching it to hold its ground, and the activating system to back off. Once it's strong enough to take its turn again, you will have it when you need it, and reaching for it in the middle of the night will start to work.
