> **STATUS (2026-06-10): refined by S2 below — read S2 first.** S1's findings (the mid-paragraph
> birth in ch05, the two-vocabulary drift, the concept-density check) all still stand. What S2
> changes is the *frame they sit in*: the ch04 wording quoted in S1 ("Underneath all four

**Prior decision (logged C1/C2):** ch04 seeds the wind-up/wind-down rhythm *unnamed*; the
names "activating/calming" arrive in ch05, on the caffeine example.

**Problem found this session:** in ch05 the pair is still being *born* mid-paragraph, inside
a paragraph whose grammatical subject is caffeine. The names arrive ~60% into a chapter about
adenosine, as a subordinate aside. Two knock-on issues:
- **Terminology drift:** the same concept carries two vocabularies introduced at two points —
  "wind up / wind down" (ch04) and "activating / calming" (ch05) — and the reader is never
  explicitly told they're the same thing (only "that wind-up from the last chapter").
- A brand-new, load-bearing term (ch06/ch07 depend on it) is introduced without its own beat.

**Recommended fix — christen in ch04, label-only, bound to wind up/wind down.** Name the pair
at its conceptual birthplace (the existing closing rhythm paragraph), fusing the two labels so
there's one vocabulary, then let ch05 simply *use* the names.

Proposed ch04 edit (existing closing rhythm paragraph):

> Underneath all four executives sits one simple rhythm. Through the day the whole system is
> built to wind up — switched on, ready to act. **Call that setting activating.** Through the
> evening it's meant to wind down — settling, recovering, making room for sleep. **Call that
> calming.** The two trade off, day and night. When that trade-off stops happening, sleep is
> the first thing to break. Hold onto **that pair** — it's the thread running through everything
> ahead.

**Concept-density check on ch04 (asked + assessed):** acceptable. This adds *labels*, not a new
concept — the wind-up/wind-down idea is already being introduced in that paragraph. It lowers
downstream density (ch05–07 stop re-gesturing at "that wind-up thing"). Two guardrails:
1. **Label-only in ch04** — no definition, no example (≈4 words added). Definition + first real
   example stay in ch05, spread out.
2. **Place ahead of the closing crescendo** ("a map of your own system…") so the chapter still
   ends on ownership, not vocabulary.
   Note: ch04 already separates the two naming systems cleanly — four *executives* (actors) vs.
   two *settings* (system states) — via "Underneath all four executives sits one simple rhythm."
   Keep that framing.

**If ch04 stays untouched (fallback):** lift the naming out of the caffeine paragraph in ch05
into its own short beat *before* caffeine, so caffeine arrives as an example of something already
named (see L7 below).

## STRUCTURAL — S2. Sleep Inc concept model (LOCKED 2026-06-10)

Agreed with author this session. This is the canonical mental model for ch04 and the
activating/calming spine. It supersedes two earlier framings *without contradicting the
underlying findings*:
- replaces **"underneath all four executives sits one simple rhythm"** (the layering was
  architecturally wrong — "underneath" already means the managers/workers rank, not where
  activating/calming lives);
- replaces the mid-session **"the company's mission is the active/calm balance"** reframe
  (a two-pole dial has no slot for Timing, which created a two-vs-three-signal seam).

**The locked model:**

1. **Mission = a good night's sleep** — the company's *output*. Produced, never forced. Must
   stay consistent with the book's downstream theme ("Sleep was downstream of everything else",
   ch04): the CEO can't pull a "sleep" lever — sleep is the result of getting conditions right.
2. **4 executives** — 2 wake (cortisol, dopamine), 2 sleep (adenosine, melatonin).
3. **Sleep happens only when the executives are aligned** — all pulling the same way at the same
   time. Misaligned = poor sleep. (Keeps the chapter's "Not broken, misaligned" payoff.)
4. **You're the CEO.** You can't command sleep or operate the executives directly — you guide
   them with **Active, Calm, and Timing** signals across the day. These three map onto ch09's
   three jobs (build the calming system / stop activation at the wrong times / keep the clock),
   so the model is consistent with the back half of the book.

**Why this resolves the earlier seams:**
- Active / Calm / Timing are now three *means to one outcome* (sleep), not poles on a shared
  metaphor. So they need not be the same kind of thing.
- The **active↔calm dial/balance** metaphor (recurs ch05 "dial pushed toward activating", ch08,
  ch09 "stuck on the activating setting", ch21) becomes a *local* device for the two opposite
  signals — it is no longer the company mission, so its silence on Timing is no contradiction.
- The "underneath the executives" architectural flaw clears: mission (sleep) → executives (how
  it's produced) → CEO guides via signals. Clean hierarchy.
- **#4 stands as written; no 5th "balance vs clock" node is needed.**

**Cautions carried into wording (do not lose when drafting):**
- a) Don't imply the CEO acts on sleep directly — keep sleep as downstream output, signals as the
  method. Framed this way, sleep-as-mission *reinforces* the downstream theme.
- b) Signals act on the **overall system / multiple executives**, not 1:1 onto one executive each.
  (Adenosine builds on its own regardless of behaviour — ch05: "almost never a shortage of
  adenosine." So there is no clean "Calm signal → adenosine" pairing.) Keep the signal→executive
  relationship loose.
- c) Keep the four executives as the *levers* the signals act through — the balance/signal frame
  must not flatten them, since ch05–08 are one-per-executive.

**Knock-on edits this model implies (to scope when wording starts):**
- Rewrite ch04's closing "rhythm" paragraph from the "underneath… wind up/wind down" version to
  the mission/signals model. The S1 naming question (where "activating/calming" gets christened)
  is now answered *inside* this model — likely at the point Active/Calm signals are introduced in
  ch04, label-only, per S1's density guardrails.
- ch04 may want the mission ("a good night's sleep", produced not forced) and the three signals
  stated nearer the *top* of the chapter, not only at the end — flag as a larger restructure, not
  a one-paragraph swap. Author aware.
- "wind up / wind down": retained as *descriptive* language where useful (it's the felt sense and
  supports ch09:14's "wind down, stop winding up" payoff); not required as a coined term once the
  Active/Calm signals carry the naming. Idiomatic uses (ch11/12/14/15/17/20/21) untouched.

**Open within S2:** exact ch04 wording, and whether mission+signals move to the chapter opening
(restructure) or stay as a reworked closing beat. Concept is locked; wording is not.

## LINE-LEVEL — ch05

### L1. Frequency drift vs ch01 ("some" → "most")
- **Location:** ch05 ¶2 — "most nights I fell asleep the moment my head hit the pillow."
- **Issue:** ch01 establishes unpredictable nights — "*Some* nights I would fall asleep as soon
  as my head hit the pillow. Others I would lie awake for hours" (ch01). ch05 upgrades to *most*,
  implying the instant-crash was the norm. The reuse of the ch01 framing as the author's flawed
  past reasoning is intentional and good; only the some→most quantifier drifts.
- **Severity:** minor. Defensible as in-character rationalising.
- **Fix:** change "most nights" → "some nights" to match ch01, or leave as deliberate exaggeration.

### L2. "one of two things… or both" logic + rhythm
- **Location:** ch05 — "The problem is usually one of two things… Or both." (and the
  `ch05-proposed` variant "one of two things. Often both.")
- **Issue:** "one of two things" sets up an exclusive choice; "or both" reopens it (really three
  outcomes). Minor logic bump; the repeated "Or" makes the tack-on feel like an afterthought.
- **Fix option:** "The problem is usually one of two things — or both. Either something is
  overriding adenosine and you can't get to sleep, or the pressure isn't clearing properly
  overnight and you wake up tired."
- **Also:** typo in /book/ ch05 — "adneonsine" → **adenosine**.

### L3. Ch7 cross-reference — VERIFIED CORRECT (no change to target)
- **Location:** ch05 — "But you lie awake anyway. That's Chapter 7."
- **Finding:** correct. Ch07 (Cortisol) is the "tired but lie awake" chapter — "When the Cortisol
  executive refuses to pack up and go home, you lie in bed with your eyes open, exhausted and
  alert" (ch07). No change to the reference.
- **Minor:** the jump "Chapter 4 introduces the other executives → That's Chapter 7" skips ch6
  (melatonin), and the lie-awake mechanism is really the ch6/ch7 *handover*. Optional smoothing:
  "…the signal gets drowned out — most often by cortisol, the one executive that won't clock off.
  …That's Chapter 7."

### L4. Disruptor list — repetition not pulling its weight
- **Location:** ch05 disruptor list (caffeine/alcohol/stress/warmth/schedule/screens).
- **Issue:** three recurring patterns dilute a list format: (a) "X requires the opposite" repeated
  (alcohol/stress/warmth); (b) the shared consequence "adenosine doesn't clear / morning heavier"
  restated in the opener, in the stress item, and again in the closer — three times; (c) uniform
  two-sentence mechanism+consequence rhythm makes longer items feel padded.
- **Fix:** put the shared consequence in the frame once; each item keeps only what's unique to it,
  then hands to its chapter. Tightened list (cuts ≈⅓, folds caffeine into the list, drops the
  "caffeine is only one of several" connector):

> Adenosine not clearing overnight comes down to one thing: not enough deep sleep. When deep
> sleep is fragmented or suppressed, the brain's overnight clearing doesn't finish. You sleep,
> but wake with the tank not quite emptied. Several things fragment deep sleep:
>
> Caffeine was my main interference. It suppressed my deep sleep more than it stopped me falling
> asleep — which is why I could drop off instantly and still wake exhausted. That's Chapter 13.
>
> Alcohol feels like a sleep aid, but it sedates you while suppressing slow-wave sleep. You were
> unconscious, not restored. That's Chapter 14.
>
> Stress holds the brain in a lighter, vigilant state — the opposite of what deep sleep needs.
> The night feels thin and restless. That's Chapter 18.
>
> Warmth works against you more than most realise: deep sleep needs your core temperature to drop,
> and a warm room makes that harder. That's Chapter 12.
>
> An irregular schedule cuts deep sleep by timing. It's front-loaded into the first part of the
> night — start late and you may not recover the full amount. That's Chapters 16 and 17.
>
> Screens don't need to be blaring. The light, notifications, and habit of checking fragment the
> lighter stages and eat into slow-wave sleep. That's Chapter 21.
>
> The common outcome is uncleared adenosine — what most of us feel as morning grogginess. It
> doesn't mean you're not a morning person. It means the clearing ran short.

- **Optional:** the dropped "If you're a coffee drinker, the full picture is…" is the one warm
  bit of repetition worth keeping if desired.

### L5. "cope with tiredness" generalisation overreaches
- **Location:** ch05 — "The thing helping you cope with tiredness is fragmenting the sleep that
  would actually fix it."
- **Issue:** fits caffeine; a stretch for alcohol; doesn't fit warmth/schedule/screens (not things
  used to cope with tiredness). Claims a shared motive only 1–2 items have.
- **Fix (accurate to the whole list):** "Whatever your disruptor, the pattern is the same. It
  costs you the deep sleep that would actually clear the tiredness — often without you noticing."
- **Fix (keeps the reversal, narrower):** "…the thing that fragments your deep sleep is often the
  very thing you're leaning on to get through the day."

### L6. "The exit isn't willpower" — unearned antagonist

- **Finding:** keep the *function* (it's the hinge into the chapter's thesis, paid off by

- **CHOSEN FIX (author selected Option A):**

### L7. Caffeine paragraph — mid-paragraph concept birth + repetition + breathing room
- **Location:** ch05 — "Caffeine is the clearest example of that wind-up from the last chapter…"
- **Issues found (in order addressed this session):**

    get their names");

  - "everything ahead turns on" reads awkward;
  - explanation too cramped (wants breathing room);

- **Best version IF naming moves to ch04 (S1 recommended path):** caffeine becomes a clean example,
  no concept birth:

> Caffeine is the clearest example. Every cup feeds the activating setting — and not for an hour.
> It keeps the dial pushed that way well into the evening, exactly when the night needs the
> opposite: calm.

- **Best version IF naming stays in ch05 (S1 fallback):** give the pair its own beat *before*
  caffeine, with room to breathe:

> Those two states from the last chapter — the daytime wind-up, the evening wind-down — are worth
> naming, because almost everything ahead comes back to them. Activating: switched on, alert.
> Calming: settling toward sleep.
>
> Caffeine is the clearest example. Every cup feeds activating — and not for an hour. It keeps the
> dial pushed that way well into the evening, exactly when the night needs the opposite: calm.

  (Replaces awkward "everything ahead turns on"; uses "these/those" anchored to ch04; trims the
  triple activate/alert.)
