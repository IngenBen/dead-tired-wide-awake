## Typos / grammar (> 1/5)

**T1 (4/5) — Line 35.** Missing apostrophe.
> And if the day was where the problem existed, ~~thats~~ **that's** where the fix had to be.

**T2 (3/5) — Spelling inconsistency: "nighttime" vs "night-time".**
Line 25 has "a nighttime problem"; line 27 has "a night-time problem". Pick one.
> But if daytime stress causes a ~~nighttime~~ **night-time** problem, what about everything else that I did during the day?

**T3 (3/5) — Spaced hyphens used as em-dashes.** Lines 27, 33, 35, 41 use " - " where the
sentence wants an em-dash (—), inconsistent with the proper em-dashes elsewhere in the
chapter (e.g. lines 5, 17, 19, 31). Examples:
> the whole system was out of alignment ~~-~~ **—** I spent all day doing the wrong things... (line 33)
> It had to be incremental ~~-~~ **—** my willpower was running on empty. (line 41)

A find-and-replace of " - " → " — " across the chapter, checked case by case, would clear it.

**T4 (3/5) — Line 27.** The folded sentence reads slightly clumsy at the join.
> Without realising it, I had been thinking about sleep entirely as a night-time problem — something with a magic switch ~~- all I had to do was find it~~ **I just had to find**. But the night was never the problem. It was just where I noticed it.

(The " - all I had to do was find it" tacks a second dash-clause onto a sentence that
already has an em-dash; folding it to "a magic switch I just had to find" keeps one clean
clause.)

**T5 (2/5) — Line 39.** Restrictive clause wants "that," not "which."
> I needed something that held my hand**,** ~~which~~ **that** worked within a modern busy schedule.

(Or drop the comma and use "that": "something that held my hand and worked within a
modern busy schedule.")
