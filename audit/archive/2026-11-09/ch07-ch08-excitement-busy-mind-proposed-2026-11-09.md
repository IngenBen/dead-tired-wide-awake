**Can looking forward to something keep you awake?**

Probably, but it has not been tested directly. Dopamine signals that something is worth pursuing, and its activity rises when a reward is signalled and builds while the outcome is awaited. In mice, silencing the dopamine-producing cells that drive this reduced wakefulness, and stimulating them woke the animals and kept them awake. In people, the wake-promoting drug modafinil raises dopamine in the brain's reward centre. Before an important competition, most elite athletes surveyed had slept worse at least once, mainly through trouble falling asleep, and most put it down to thinking about the event; the survey did not ask about excitement separately from nerves. No study has measured dopamine in people lying awake looking forward to something, so the link is the book's reasoning from these findings.

Berridge KC 2007, *Psychopharmacology* 191:391–431. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-006-0578-x
Fiorillo CD, Tobler PN, Schultz W 2003, *Science* 299:1898–1902. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1077349 (recordings in monkeys; activity rose to signals that predicted a reward and built gradually until the reward might arrive)
Eban-Rothschild A, Rothschild G, Giardino WJ, Jones JR, de Lecea L 2016, *Nat Neurosci* 19:1356–1366. https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4377 (mice; inhibiting dopamine cells in the ventral tegmental area reduced wakefulness even with food, a mate or predator odour present, and stimulating them started and maintained wakefulness)
Volkow ND et al. 2009, *JAMA* 301(11):1148–1154. https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2009.351 (brain scans of 10 healthy men; therapeutic doses of modafinil blocked dopamine transporters and raised dopamine, most in the nucleus accumbens; modafinil also acts on other wake-promoting systems)
Juliff LE, Halson SL, Peiffer JJ 2015, *J Sci Med Sport* 18:13–18. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsams.2014.02.007 (survey of 283 elite Australian athletes; 64% had slept worse before an important competition at least once, mostly difficulty falling asleep; the reasons offered were thoughts about the competition (84%) and nervousness (44%), with no option for excitement)
