# Ch20 nasal breathing: prose proposal and endnote change

Proposal only. /book/ is not edited; apply by hand. Additions in **bold**, removals struck through.
Written 2026-10-28 at Ben's request: add the stronger reason for nasal breathing in meditation, and retitle the endnote "Is nasal breathing better for meditation?".

## Evidence checked

New sources collected 2026-10-28 (derived/sources/, status COLLECTED):

- **S208 Watso 2023** (20 young adults, randomised crossover, same breathing pace): at rest, nose breathing slightly lowered diastolic blood pressure and raised the calming (parasympathetic) share of heart-rate variability vs mouth breathing. No benefit during light cycling.
- **S209 Zaccaro 2022** (12 experienced meditators, very slow breathing, randomised crossover): after nose breathing, less anxiety, more joy, deeper meditative state and more slow-wave brain activity than after the same breathing through the mouth. Heart-rate variability did NOT differ. Unblinded; several results borderline.
- **S210 Zelano 2016** (brain recordings in 7 patients): activity in smell, emotion and memory areas follows nasal breathing and fades with mouth breathing. Did not measure calm.

Verdict: factual as a modest claim ("seems to", "in small studies"). Not strong enough for an unqualified claim that nose breathing calms the body. The heart-rhythm evidence is mixed across the two studies; the anxiety/mood evidence is from one small study.

Also corrected: the existing line says nasal breathing "naturally slows the breath down". The note's own sources show it does not change breathing rate at rest by itself; it limits airflow, which helps a deliberately slow breath. The proposal softens this to "helps you slow down".

It's** much harder to over-breathe through your nose than through your mouth**, and a slow, deep breath simply takes longer to draw in**.
