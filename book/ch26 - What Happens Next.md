# PART 4 – Over to you

# Chapter 26 - What Happens Next

Most people start in the same place. Sleep is a night-time problem, fixed at night, by trying harder at night. Better willpower. Earlier bedtime. The right supplement. Something that works on the night itself, because that's where the problem seems to live.

That's where I started.

The program starts from a different idea. You are not trying to fix sleep directly. You are creating the conditions that allow it to happen. Nobody tries to get fit on the night of a race. You build the fitness first, and the performance follows. Sleep works the same way. Lots of small daily habits, done consistently. That shift (from chasing the outcome to supporting the system that produces it) is the whole thing.

The habits were doing three things: training your body clock, building your calming system, and removing the disruptors that kept you awake at night, so sleep could come when it should. Each one seemed almost too small to matter: light exposure in the morning, a three-hour gap between dinner and bed, a consistent wake time. None of it felt like fixing sleep. That was the point.

The progress is gradual, but recognising it can happen in a moment.

There's no single morning where the problem is suddenly "fixed". What happens instead is quieter, and it adds up. Falling asleep becomes slightly less effortful. You notice you've stopped watching the clock. A run of good nights starts to feel normal rather than lucky. And then, at some point, it becomes undeniable: the change you'd been waiting for is real. The bad nights still come. They always will. But they stop defining you as a bad sleeper. Sleep stops being something you think about with anxiety.

You've come a long way to get here. You've read the book. You know more now than when you started. But knowledge doesn't improve your sleep. Action does. And the gap between the two is where a lot of people get stuck.

It doesn't matter whether it feels manageable or overwhelming right now. You don't need to do everything. You need to do something. Then the next thing. Then the thing after that.

Everything you need is already in your hands. That said, if you'd like extra help, there's a free app to track your progress, and the **Sleep Builders** community to ask questions and share what's working. You'll find both at www.sleepbuilders.com.au. You don't need it. But it's there.

Either way, start with the foundational stuff. Order a couple of things online. Set up your environment. Then pick the first habit that feels achievable. Build it until it's automatic. Then pick the next one. You already know what to do. You've just read it. The only thing left is to begin.

If you stay with it, something will shift. The nights will become more reliable. The mornings will feel different. Sleep will stop being something you lie awake hoping for and start being something you can count on. Not every night. But most nights. In a real life, with family and a full schedule and everything else that doesn't stop just because you're trying to sleep better.

Most nights is enough.

When my wife read the first draft chapters of this book, she came downstairs a little teary. She gave me a hug and said: it really is a lot you've been through. It's amazing you're still here. I told her I had a big rock to lean on. It was amazing she'd stuck with me through it all. She said: I knew you were still in there.

That was the greatest success of all. Not sleep scores. Not optimising protocols. Having someone you care about deeply notice the change in you. Becoming present for the people who have been waiting for you to return.

I wish you every success with it. This approach made a change in my life I didn't believe was possible. That's the reason I wrote it: the hope that it might do the same for someone else.

From here, the book has done what it can. You're the CEO of Sleep Incorporated. You understand how the company runs now. And you have the training program to get the four executives working together. They'll turn the company around.

The mind that wouldn't switch off finally can. 

Now go get some sleep.
