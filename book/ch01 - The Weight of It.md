# Chapter 1 - The Weight of It

This was me.

Tired but wired. A terrible sleeper. No energy. A constantly busy mind. The nights I wanted sleep, it wouldn't come. The moments I needed to be present, I was struggling to keep my eyes open. 

Despite being a glass-half-full kind of person, I was often depressed or moody. I learned to mask it. Lacking willpower and motivation, I pushed through on self-talk alone. Craving sugar and caffeine. Getting irritated by things that shouldn't have bothered me. I was good at filtering what came out… most of the time. It rarely boiled over into anger. It usually surfaced as short snappy comments, or more typically withdrawing myself before I said something I would regret.

Trying to cope was deeply exhausting.

Other times, when I wasn't dealing with irritability, I just felt detached. Like I was on a different wavelength. I'd say or do something I thought might get a laugh, and it would land as weird, or just plain mean. Later I'd look back and wonder what I was thinking.

All the masking, the pushing through, the filtering, the disconnection, and all the while life never stopped. At times it was completely overwhelming. Keeping up felt like driving down the highway with the handbrake on.

The weight was killing me. Figuratively, in the day to day, but literally too. I knew poor sleep sat behind some very serious long-term diseases.

The nights were unpredictable. Some nights I would fall asleep as soon as my head hit the pillow. Others I would lie awake for hours. But either way I would wake up several times. And getting back to sleep, when it came at all, took a long time. I tried compensating by spending nine or more hours in bed. But I was still only getting four or five hours of actual sleep. Broken and poor quality. Waking up only slightly less tired than when I went to bed.

You would think, on the nights it happened, falling asleep the moment my head hit the pillow was a good thing. It wasn't. It meant I was running such a huge sleep deficit my body would just crash.

The days were their own kind of punishment. I spent most of them feeling like I could fall asleep at any moment. Particularly after meals, and in the dreaded afternoon dip, that stretch of mid-afternoon where the fatigue hit like a wall. I tried to nap whenever I could, mostly on weekends and while commuting to work. These helped, but a semi-lucid 15 minute rest was usually all I could achieve. Sugar and caffeine filled the gap and got me through… until they didn't.

Unless you've lived it, it's hard to understand. I'd smile to myself when someone said, "God, I'm wrecked today, the fire alarm went off last night and it took me two hours to get back to sleep." That was their worst night in memory. It was better than most of mine. Part of the problem was that I couldn't even describe what was wrong. My thoughts were so scattered that "I can't sleep" was about as far as I could get.

After years of trying and failing, I started to wonder whether the problem was just me.

I had recurring dreams, too. The setting was always different, but the theme never changed. Most dreams are unrealistic enough that when I woke, my rational mind took over and settled me, I knew none of it had been real. These weren't like that. They were set in ordinary, everyday life, which made them hard to shake. Clubs that wouldn't let me in. Friends making plans without me. Parties I wasn't invited to. Jokes I wasn't in on. The theme was always the same: you're not good enough. With hindsight, I believe this was a subconscious reflection of a ever present feeling of not keep up with life. 

I so desperately wanted this problem to be 'one thing'. A vitamin deficiency. A parasite. Some disease with a name and a cure. Something I could point to and fix. A single villain to defeat. But there was never anything to find.

Maybe it was genetic. Maybe it was just how I was built. I had a busy mind. Things hit me harder. Perhaps I needed to stop fighting it and learn to live with it, accept it as my cross to bear, and put my energy into everything else that was good in life.

But there was a part of me that wouldn't quite give up. I wish I could say it was some kind of Zen-like intuition. An admirable unwavering dedication. It wasn't. It was more like a stubborn refusal to accept there was nothing I could do.

It was more like crawling through the desert. Exhausted and desperate, angry no one was coming to get me. Begrudgingly putting one heavy foot in front of the other, not knowing whether I'd make it out. But not having the option to give up entirely.

During the darkest stretches, thoughts would surface that frightened me. I knew I'd never act on them, but the fact they were showing up at all told me how badly this was affecting me.

There were times where I would stop searching for a while. But I couldn't quite accept that this was just going to be my life. That I'd feel this way for the rest of it.

So I kept going, because stopping felt worse. There was just too much I didn't want to miss.

I didn't want to be a spectator of my own life - wanting to join in, but never quite having the energy to. Every birthday feeling like another year had slipped past, in a life less than it could be.

Often I would sit with my kids in the evening, reading to them before bed. It should have been a good part of the day. But I was always foggy, barely able to follow the story. I would read aloud and not take it in. My children were right there, tucked into me, and I was just trying to keep my eyes open long enough to get to the end of the chapter.

That was the whole problem. It took over every important moment of my life.

I didn't know if things would ever change.
