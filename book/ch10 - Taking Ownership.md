# PART 3 – The Program

# Chapter 10 - Taking Ownership

I wasn't the problem. But I was the solution. I was the CEO of Sleep Incorporated, which meant it was mine to turn around.

That felt heavy but understanding it was actually a relief. I'd spent years looking for something outside myself to fix it. A pill. A routine I'd read about somewhere. A YouTube expert with the one thing I was missing. None of it stuck, because none of it had addressed the whole company.

Once I stopped waiting to be rescued and started running the company myself, I got to work on a training program.

For most of my life I'd been stuck on the day shift: wound up, switched on, a system that never clocked off. What was missing was the handover: a calming system strong enough to bring the night shift on.

The two shifts are built to take turns, and bad sleep almost always comes down to one of two things: a handover that never happens, or the two swapping on the wrong schedule. Both can be trained, through the signals you send every day.

So the habits do the three jobs from Chapter 4, turned into daily action. Train the body clock. Build the calming system. Remove the disruptors that get in the way. Every habit in the chapters ahead is one of those three. Know each habit's job and you'll see how it works toward a good night's sleep.

The program runs in order. Twelve habits, each building on the last. Ready for you to work through at your own pace.

Each habit draws on sleep research, the same science we walked through in the last four chapters. Where the evidence is thinner or still debated, the Notes at the back say so.

What I've done is curate. Determined the sequence. Determined how much to do and when. Worked out what can flex and still give results. And most importantly built it to fit into a modern lifestyle: full-time job, family, exercise, and the general busyness of a life.

I could easily give you twice as many habits, and they'd all have research behind them. But that's not a program. That's a list. And a list is easy to close and never look at again.

Massage, for example, didn't make the cut. There's research behind it. But the cost and time away from the kids' dinner and bed routine just wasn't realistic for me.

The question for each habit was the same: what does science say is ideal, what's realistically achievable most of the time, and do I still get results if I aim for that?

I very deliberately sacrificed science-optimal for life-optimal. Because a habit you sustain beats a perfect protocol you abandon in a couple of weeks. The habits in this program are the ones that survived that test.

Your schedule and family life won't be exactly the same as mine. But I hope to share what I did, help you understand why, and encourage you to be imperfect and adjust to suit your circumstances.

Some of these habits you might already be doing. If so, great. Cross them off and take the win. For the rest, go slowly. Introduce one or two habits at a time. If they feel easy, add a couple more. If not, pause and work on these until they do. Slow changes that stick beat fast ones that don't.

A fair question before you start is: how long? It depends on where you're starting and how quickly you move. The program runs in phases. Move at roughly a phase a week and you could be through them in five weeks; if you already have some of these habits in place, then maybe faster. Go slower and that's fine too.

But the moment worth waiting for comes before the end. It's the first time the change becomes undeniable: not "I think I feel better", but "something has definitely shifted". For me, that arrived a week or two after the habits in phase 4 had bedded in, the first nights my calming system did the work on its own, without me forcing it. That's what breaks the old story: I'll never be a good sleeper. Once it comes, your motivation to finish the remaining habits takes care of itself.

One last thing before Phase 1 begins.

I strongly recommend recording whatever you're working on, with regular self-check-ins. The easiest way is the app at www.sleepbuilders.com.au. It's free, so I'd encourage you to use it. It'll help you navigate the program, track where you're at, and prompt those check-ins. If you're like me, you won't always do everything, every day. I needed reminding and reinforcement.

If an app isn't for you, paper works just as well. A simple list, phases marked off, habits ticked as they land. A few notes-to-self about progress. What matters is that you do it. Writing things down helps with commitment.

Either way, app or paper, what I'd encourage most is joining others doing the program. People are far more likely to see it through if they are working the same habits together, answering questions and helping each other through challenges. It's worth joining the community. It's called **Sleep Builders**, and you'll find it at the same URL, www.sleepbuilders.com.au.

That's it. You now know everything you need to know before we begin. There's nothing left to do but turn to Chapter 11, and let's get to work.
