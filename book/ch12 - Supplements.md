# Chapter 12 - Supplements

For a long time, supplements were my hope for a silver bullet. I'd find something that promised deep, restorative sleep, read enough five-star reviews to convince myself this was the one, order it, and wait. It usually turned out just like my experience with CBN. Never seemed consistent. Typically, I'd move on within a couple of weeks and start the search again.

Once I started tracking sleep, eventually I found a few that did work. They still didn't give me 'the best sleep ever' that many claim. But tracking showed meaningful increases in deep sleep and total sleep.

**Not all supplements are the same**

Before I get to the specific supplements, there is one thing worth knowing. Most supplements share a generic ingredient name. For example Magnesium. But often within those generic names, the form or extract makes a significant difference to how well the body absorbs it and what it actually does.

I spent money on the wrong forms before I understood this. The generic magnesium I started with did very little. When I switched to the specific form I mention below, the difference was noticeable.

**What I take and why**
You met cortisol back in Chapter 7, the hormone meant to fall away through the evening but, for most of us, refuses to. Once I understood that, my search narrowed to one question: which supplements target cortisol? That left only a handful to test, and after some trial and error, I landed on these three.

The first is Phosphatidylserine, sometimes marketed as PS. It's a type of lipid that exists naturally in the brain and appears to blunt the evening cortisol response. The form to look for is soy- or sunflower-derived. The trial dose was 800 milligrams a day. My suggestion is around 400 milligrams, taken 30–60 minutes before bed.

The second is Ashwagandha. This one has been around for centuries and has some solid modern research. The extract with the most evidence behind it is called KSM-66. That's a specific form of ashwagandha root extract, standardised to contain a minimum concentration. If the label just says "ashwagandha" without specifying an extract like KSM-66, you don't really know what you're getting. The dosage range is 300–600 milligrams. The trials typically split it across the day, morning and evening. Its primary effect for sleep is reducing the stress response, making the evenings a little calmer.

The third is Magnesium. This is the most well-known of the three. Surprisingly, though, the research is thinner than you'd expect: there aren't many good studies that prove it works. But it's recommended everywhere, from sleep forums to health articles, and my own experience backs that up. For sleep, the form worth looking for is Magnesium L-threonate, often recommended on the basis that it crosses into the brain more readily. Magnesium citrate and glycinate are reasonable alternatives if L-threonate isn't available. Dosing depends on the form. For L-threonate, the studied dose was 1.5 to 2 grams of the compound a day, depending on body weight. At 2 grams that delivers only around 144 milligrams of elemental magnesium. For citrate or glycinate, trials gave 320 to 729 milligrams of elemental magnesium a day, split across two or three doses. Roughly 150 to 300 milligrams in the evening fits within that range per dose, though it's my suggestion rather than a tested bedtime dose.

There are other effective supplements out there. But as I cycled through them, some gave me side effects. Typically disturbing dreams or digestive issues.

For quick reference:

| Supplement              | Form to look for                                   | Dosage                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Phosphatidylserine (PS) | Soy- or sunflower-derived                          | ~400 mg (my suggestion; trial used 800 mg/day)                                                                                 |
| Ashwagandha             | KSM-66 extract                                     | 300–600 mg                                                                                                                     |

**What the data actually showed**

I used the same on/off approach I described in the last chapter: cycling each supplement, watching the tracker, looking for improvements.

Some of what I found surprised me. Certain supplements improved my deep sleep measurably. I could see it in the staging data, trending upward over the weeks I was taking them. Others I tried along the way brought side effects I hadn't anticipated. Vivid, unsettling dreams on some nights. Occasional heart rate spikes that showed up in the overnight data and disappeared when I stopped taking them. Nothing dangerous, but enough to make me drop and keep looking. The three I've landed on didn't give me those problems. They just did what I needed them to do.

This is why the tracker matters for this part of the program specifically. Supplements affect people differently. The dosage ranges I've given are starting points, not prescriptions. What the data showed me was my own response, and without the tracker, I'd have had no way to know whether what I was feeling was real or imagined.

**Why I stopped looking for more**

My goal was always to keep things as simple as possible: the minimum number of supplements for the maximum benefit. Cortisol is often the biggest sleep problem, and all three target it from a different angle. So I stopped. Not because nothing else out there works, but because more would only make the routine harder to keep.

**When I take them**

I don't follow those timings exactly.

I take all three after dinner every night. A routine that I'll actually follow beats a routine that's theoretically optimal. An overwhelmed, sleep-deprived brain does not need another thing to remember at different times of the evening.

I have my little routine. Finish dinner, take my plate, get my supplements. Done.

When you get started, don't take all three at once. If one doesn't agree with you (an unsettled stomach, disrupted dreams, anything that feels off) it's easier to identify which one.

Layer them in instead. Start with one supplement for two days. Then add the second for the next two days. Then add the third. By the end of the week you'll be taking all three after dinner.

Better sleep may not immediately show on your sleep tracker this early on. That's OK. We're just getting started. Supplements are a support, not the main event. As the other habits in this book fall into place, they give the supplements more room to do their work. Keep them in the routine and let progress build.

If something doesn't feel right at any point, stop that supplement, let things settle, and reintroduce it at a lower dose or try a different form.

Three supplements. Consistent timing. Trackable results. That's the whole supplement strategy.
