## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- In rats, overtraining a stimulus-response behaviour reliably produces inflexible habits.
- Only one human study had shown that extensive training increases habits.
- Five attempts using variants of four published outcome-devaluation tasks, including two replications of that study, found extensive training did not increase habits.
- Authors suggest these lab tasks may not be sensitive to habit strength in humans.

### Lab Training Fails to Create Habits in People

In 2018, an international team of researchers reported five experiments that tried to make healthy adult volunteers form habits in the lab by having them practise the same responses for much longer.

In rats, lots of practice reliably turns actions into rigid habits. In people, it did not, in any of the five attempts, including two attempts to repeat the one earlier study that had seemed to show it.

**The main takeaway:** Habits are hard to create and measure in short human lab studies, so lab-based claims about habits in people rest on shaky methods.
