## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- **Design:** randomised, double-blind, crossover. 6 weeks' baseline on usual diet, then 6 weeks on one diet, a 2-week break, and 6 weeks on the other. Elimination diet (IgG-positive foods removed) vs provocation diet (IgG-positive foods included). Diets set by a dietitian and calorie-matched; patients and the headache doctor blinded to diet type and test results.
- **Participants:** 35 enrolled, 30 completed (28 women, 2 men; aged 19–52, mean 35); migraine without aura, at least 4 attacks and 4 headache days a month; migraine for 13 years on average. All used acute medication; half also used preventive medication.
- **Test:** ImuPro 300 ELISA, IgG to 266 foods; on average 24 foods flagged per patient (range 7–47). Not repeated after the diets.
- **Elimination vs baseline:** attacks fell from about 9.0 to 6.2 and headache days from about 10.5 to 7.5 over the period; attacks needing medication and tablets taken also fell. Provocation vs baseline: no significant change.
- **Elimination vs provocation (change from baseline):** attacks −29% vs −8%; headache days −26% vs −3%; medication use also favoured elimination. Attack severity and duration did not differ.
- **Responders:** about half had at least a 30% drop in headache days or attacks on elimination; about one in five had at least a 50% drop. No order effect.
- **Authors' conclusion:** IgG-based diet restriction might help reduce migraine frequency, perhaps in people whose migraines resist treatment; small sample, so larger studies needed.

### IgG-Guided Diet Cut Migraine Days by About a Quarter

In a 2010 study at Istanbul Faculty of Medicine in Turkey, researchers tested 30 adults with regular migraine, almost all of them women, for IgG antibodies to 266 foods.

After six weeks on their usual diet, each person spent six weeks on a diet that cut out their flagged foods and, after a two-week break, six weeks on a diet that kept them in. A dietitian designed both diets, and neither the patients nor their headache doctor knew which was which. Everyone kept a headache diary.

While avoiding their flagged foods, people had about a quarter fewer headache days and migraine attacks than at the start, and took less medication. On the diet that kept the foods in, there was little change. The migraines that did come were no milder or shorter.

**The main takeaway:** For people with migraine, cutting out the foods an IgG test flagged reduced how often attacks came, though not how bad they were.
