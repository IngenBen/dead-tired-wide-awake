## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- **Design:** 34 healthy adults (16 men, 18 women; mean age 25.3, range 18–42). Each did two 5-day lab sessions, each after 7–9 days of fixed sleep at home. Between dim-light melatonin onset (DLMO) measurements were 3 days on an ultradian cycle (2.5 h awake in dim room light, 1.5 h in the dark to sleep), so the clock free-ran without the usual 24-hour cues. One pill a day at a fixed clock time: 0.5 mg immediate-release melatonin in one session, placebo in the other (double blind, counterbalanced). Groups of 3–4 took the pill at different times to cover the whole 24-hour day. The phase shift on placebo was subtracted from the shift on melatonin.
- **Comparison:** the 0.5 mg curve was compared with the team's 3.0 mg curve from an earlier study (Burgess, Revell & Eastman 2008, *J Physiol* 586:639–647), made with the same protocol but different people.
- **0.5 mg results:** the largest advances came when it was taken in the afternoon, 2–4 h before DLMO (fitted peak 3.3 h before DLMO), or 9–11 h before mid-sleep. Fitted maximum advance 1.5 h, maximum delay 1.3 h (1.6 h on the sleep-midpoint curve), over 3 days. Some individuals advanced by up to 2.9 h. The data were more scattered than for 3.0 mg.
- **0.5 vs 3.0 mg:** peak-to-trough size 2.8 h for 0.5 mg vs 2.9 h for 3.0 mg. Advances and delays were similar in size (P > 0.60 for both). The best times were about 2 h later for 0.5 mg (advances P = 0.039, delays P = 0.046). Relative to mid-sleep, the best advance time was about 10 h before for 0.5 mg and 11 h before for 3.0 mg.
- **Bedtime use:** both curves show little clock effect when melatonin is taken at bedtime, as a sleep aid. Melatonin taken near the end of sleep may delay the clock, especially at 3.0 mg.
- **Dose interpretation:** the authors suggest that above physiological levels, phase shifting may be roughly all-or-none, so any dose from 0.5 mg up gives the same-sized shift if taken at its own best time. Earlier dose-response findings (0.05, 0.5, 5 mg given at the same time gave advances of 0.4, 0.7, 1.4 h) may reflect that time suiting the larger dose. They also say a fixed time over 3 days may have missed each dose's true maximum.
- **Authors' practical advice:** 3.0 mg is more likely to produce a substantial shift than 0.5 mg, but 0.5 mg is more appropriate where the drowsiness of 3.0 mg needs avoiding.
- **Pill content:** tested 0.5 mg pills held 0.5–0.8 mg; 3.0 mg pills held 2.4–7.8 mg.
- **Funding:** NIH grant R01 HL086934. Melatonin and placebo pills donated by Ecological Formulas. Authors declared nothing to disclose.

### Half a Milligram Shifts the Body Clock Like Three

In a 2010 study at Rush University in Chicago, USA, researchers brought 34 healthy young men and women into a lab twice for five days. In between measurements of when their melatonin naturally switched on, they lived in dim light on a short cycle of naps and waking, so their clocks drifted freely, and they took one pill a day at the same time: 0.5 mg melatonin one visit, a dummy pill the other, without knowing which. Different small groups took their pill at different times of day.

Taken in the afternoon, a few hours before the body's own melatonin would rise, 0.5 mg moved the clock about an hour and a half earlier over three days. That's the same size of shift the team had found for 3 mg in an earlier study using the same method. The small dose simply worked best about two hours later in the day. Neither dose did much to the clock when taken at bedtime.

**The main takeaway:** For shifting the body clock, 0.5 mg moves it about as far as 3 mg, as long as it's taken at the right time. The low dose's best time is a little later in the afternoon or evening.

- **Authors:** Burgess HJ, Revell VL, Molina TA, Eastman CI
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2009-2590
- **Published in:** *Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism* (peer reviewed)
- **Study type:** Randomised double-blind placebo-controlled crossover lab trial (phase response curve)
- **Age range:** 18–42
- **Measures:** Timing of melatonin onset in saliva under dim light, before and after 3 days of pills
- **Funding:** NIH; pills donated by a supplement maker
- **Caveat:** The 3 mg results come from an earlier study in different people; sleep itself was not measured; healthy young adults with free-running clocks, not real-world jet lag; the authors still lean to 3 mg for a reliable shift
- **Replication:** Peak advance timing matches the Lewy 1998 0.5 mg curve; consistent with Mundey 2005
- **Evidence:** Strong for clock shifting (carefully controlled); silent on sleep
