## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- Habits set up a split between conscious, deliberate control of behaviour and lower-level control that is barely available to awareness.
- Many routines and rituals become habitual and stereotyped through learning; some have a strong innate basis.
- The review proposes these behaviours emerge from experience-dependent plasticity in basal ganglia circuits, which can shape thinking as well as actions.

### Habits Live in the Brain's Basal Ganglia

In a 2008 review at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in the USA, neuroscientist Ann Graybiel drew together research, much of it in animals, on how routines become automatic.

She argues that repeated behaviours become fixed through learning-driven changes in the basal ganglia, a set of deep brain structures, and that these circuits can run behaviour with little conscious involvement. The same machinery may underlie compulsions and addictive behaviour.

**The main takeaway:** Repetition hands behaviour over to deep brain circuits that run it largely outside awareness.
