### Little Evidence That Self-Control Runs on Sugar

In a 2016 analysis, researchers Miguel Vadillo, Natalie Gold and Magda Osman re-examined the experiments behind a popular idea: that self-control uses up blood sugar, and a sugary drink tops it back up.

Using a statistical check of how trustworthy a set of published results is, they found the evidence weak and likely inflated by the tendency to publish only positive findings.

**The main takeaway:** The idea that willpower is fuelled by blood sugar is not well supported.
