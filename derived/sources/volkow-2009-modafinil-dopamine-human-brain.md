## Abstract (summarised, not copied)

Pilot PET study (2007–2008, Brookhaven National Laboratory) in 10 healthy men, each scanned after placebo and after oral modafinil (200 or 400 mg). [11C]raclopride binding, a marker of extracellular dopamine, fell in the caudate (6.1%), putamen (6.7%) and nucleus accumbens (19.4%), indicating more dopamine. [11C]cocaine binding fell by 39–54% across these regions, indicating that modafinil occupied dopamine transporters. The authors conclude modafinil raises dopamine in the human brain at therapeutic doses and call for awareness of abuse potential.

### A Wake-Promoting Drug Raises Dopamine in the Human Brain

In a 2009 study at Brookhaven National Laboratory in the US, researchers scanned the brains of ten healthy men after a placebo and after a normal dose of modafinil, a drug prescribed to keep people awake. Modafinil blocked the pumps that clear dopamine away and raised dopamine levels, most of all in the brain's reward centre.

**The main takeaway:** A drug used to keep people awake works at least partly by boosting dopamine, linking dopamine to wakefulness in humans.
