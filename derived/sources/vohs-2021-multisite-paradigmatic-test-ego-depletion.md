## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- 36 labs, N = 3,531; each lab used one of two procedures designed to tire self-control, then a second self-control measure.
- Confirmatory test non-significant (d = 0.06); Bayesian analysis found the data about four times more likely under no effect than under the expected effect.
- Exploratory analysis of everyone, ignoring exclusions, found a tiny effect (d = 0.08), with the evidence about evenly balanced.
- Effect possibly larger in people reporting more fatigue; not affected by trait self-control or beliefs about willpower.

### Even Its Supporters Couldn't Find Willpower Depletion

In a 2021 project led from the University of Minnesota in the USA, 36 laboratories tested the 'willpower runs out' idea with more than 3,500 adults, this time using methods chosen by the idea's own supporters.

Participants did a task meant to tire their self-control, or an easier one, and then a second self-control task.

The planned analysis found no reliable effect. The data fitted 'no effect' about four times better than the effect the researchers had expected.

**The main takeaway:** Large, careful tests, including by its own champions, have not found that self-control reliably runs down like a battery.
