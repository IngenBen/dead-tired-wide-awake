## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- Participants chose between money rewards that differed in how long they would have to wait for them, while in a brain scanner.
- Limbic areas linked to the midbrain dopamine system responded preferentially to choices involving immediately available rewards.
- Lateral prefrontal and posterior parietal areas were engaged by all such choices, whatever the delay.
- Greater relative prefrontal-parietal activity went with choosing the longer-term option.

### Reward Brain Lights Up for Now, Planning Brain for Later

In a 2004 study at Princeton University in the USA, researchers scanned the brains of adult volunteers while they chose between smaller amounts of money sooner and larger amounts later.

Reward-related areas tied to dopamine responded most when one option was available straight away. Planning areas at the front and side of the brain were active for every choice. When planning areas were relatively more active, people were more likely to wait for the bigger, later reward.

**The main takeaway:** Immediate rewards get a special boost in the brain's reward circuitry, and choosing to wait goes with more activity in planning areas.
