## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- **Design:** randomised controlled trial; 150 outpatients with irritable bowel syndrome. IgG antibodies to foods measured by ELISA. True diet: all foods with raised IgG excluded. Sham diet: the same number of foods excluded, none with raised IgG. Three months (12 weeks). Intention-to-treat analysis.
- **Primary outcomes:** IBS symptom severity score and global rating. **Secondary:** non-bowel symptoms, quality of life, anxiety and depression.
- **Symptom severity:** true diet reduced scores about 10% more than sham (mean difference 39 points, 95% CI 5–72). In fully compliant patients the difference was about 26% (98 points, 95% CI 52–144).
- **Global rating:** improved more on the true diet (number needed to treat 9; 2.5 in compliant patients).
- **Secondary outcomes:** all trended in favour of the true diet; no figures given in the abstract.
- **Relaxing the diet:** symptoms worsened about 24% more in the true-diet group (difference 52, 95% CI 18–88).
- **Authors' conclusion:** IgG-based elimination may reduce IBS symptoms and is worth further research.

### Cutting IgG-Flagged Foods Eased IBS More Than a Fake Diet

In a 2004 study at hospitals in Manchester and York in the UK, researchers took blood from 150 adults with irritable bowel syndrome and tested it for IgG antibodies to a range of foods.

Half were then given a diet cutting out every food their test flagged. The other half got a decoy diet that cut out the same number of foods, but none that had been flagged. Both groups followed their diet for three months and rated their bowel symptoms and how they felt overall.

The real diet did better. Symptom scores fell about 10% more than on the decoy diet, and in people who stuck to it fully, about 26% more. When people later relaxed the diet, symptoms came back more in the real-diet group. Mood, quality of life and non-bowel symptoms also leaned in the real diet's favour, though less clearly.

**The main takeaway:** For people with IBS, avoiding the foods an IgG test flags eased symptoms more than avoiding the same number of random foods, especially for people who stuck with it.
