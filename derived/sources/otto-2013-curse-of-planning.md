## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- Participants made sequential choices while sometimes doing a demanding secondary task.
- Under the extra load, they relied more on a habit-like ('model-free') strategy and less on deliberate planning ('model-based').
- People shifted between strategies trial by trial as demands changed, and response times reflected the cost of the strategy used.
- Authors conclude cognitive resources set the balance between the two systems.

### When the Mind Is Busy, Habits Take Over

In a 2013 study, researchers had adult volunteers play a decision game that could be played either by thinking ahead or by repeating what had paid off before.

On some turns, they also had to do a demanding second task. When their minds were taken up by the extra task, people switched towards simply repeating what had worked before, and went back to planning when the load lifted.

**The main takeaway:** Deliberate planning needs spare mental capacity; when it is used up elsewhere, people fall back on habit-like responses.
