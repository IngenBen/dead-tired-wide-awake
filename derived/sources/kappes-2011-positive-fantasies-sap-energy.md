## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- Study 1: 164 female students imagined wearing high heels either glamorously (positive) or questioningly. Positive fantasies lowered systolic blood pressure slightly; questioning ones did not.
- Study 2: 50 students imagined winning an essay prize with everything going well vs with doubts. Positive fantasies gave lower self-reported energy.
- Study 3: 40 students imagined an ideal week vs neutral thoughts. Positive fantasies lowered energy and, a week later, reported accomplishment; lower energy explained the drop.
- Study 4: 80 students, some made thirsty with salty crackers, imagined academic success or a drink. Fantasies about the thing they currently needed lowered blood pressure most.
- Limitations: small, mostly female student samples; short lab tasks; energy not measured as actual effort; one study had dropout.

### Daydreaming About Success Drains Your Energy

In 2011, psychologists Heather Kappes and Gabriele Oettingen ran four small experiments with university students, most of them young women.

Students were asked to imagine a future in a glowing, everything-goes-right way, or in a more questioning or neutral way: wearing glamorous shoes, winning an essay prize, having a great week, or getting a drink when thirsty. Their blood pressure was taken before and after, or they rated how energetic they felt.

Glowing fantasies left people slightly calmer and less energised. In the one study that followed people up, those who had imagined an ideal week reported getting less done that week.

**The main takeaway:** Picturing a goal as already achieved can relax you instead of firing you up, leaving less drive to work towards it.
