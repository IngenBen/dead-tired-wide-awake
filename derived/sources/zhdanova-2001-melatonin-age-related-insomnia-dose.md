## Abstract (close summary, not copied text)

Double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial in 30 adults over 50: 15 with insomnia (actigraphy: sleep efficiency below 85% or under 6 h sleep on 3 of 7 nights; 11 of 15 confirmed by polysomnography) and 15 age- and sex-matched normal sleepers. Each took placebo, 0.1, 0.3 and 3.0 mg melatonin 30 minutes before bedtime for one week each, with one-week washouts (9 weeks in all). Sleep was measured by polysomnography on inpatient nights at the end of each treatment week, plus actigraphy at home. In insomniacs, 0.3 mg gave the largest gain in sleep efficiency (P < 0.0001), mainly in the middle third of the night. Normal sleepers did not change. The authors describe 3.0 mg as somewhat less effective and possibly less effective than the physiological dose.

No dose-related effects on sleep latency, total sleep time, awakenings, REM latency or sleep stages were reported as significant. 3.0 mg lowered minimum core body temperature (P < 0.0001); 0.1 and 0.3 mg did not. Median peak plasma melatonin was 1370 pg/ml after 3.0 mg vs 220 pg/ml after 0.3 mg; after 3.0 mg levels stayed raised into the next day, while 0.1 and 0.3 mg were back to placebo levels by 10 h after bedtime. Subjective sleep reports were collected but no results are given. Funding: NIH grants and the Center for Brain Sciences and Metabolism Charitable Trust.

### A Tiny Melatonin Dose Beat a Big One in Insomnia

In a 2001 study at MIT in the USA, researchers gave 30 adults over 50, half with insomnia and half sleeping normally, a week each of a dummy pill and three melatonin doses: 0.1, 0.3 and 3 mg, taken half an hour before bed. Nobody knew which they had. At the end of each week they slept in the lab with their sleep measured.

For the people with insomnia, the 0.3 mg dose (close to what the body makes itself) helped most: they spent 88% of the night asleep, against 78% on the dummy pill and 84% on 3 mg. The gain was in the middle of the night, not at the start: none of the doses changed how quickly anyone fell asleep, and the insomnia group was already falling asleep in about 10 minutes. Normal sleepers didn't change at all. The 3 mg dose lowered body temperature overnight and left melatonin raised into the next day.

**The main takeaway:** For older people who wake in the night, a small dose near the body's own level helped staying asleep as much as or more than a dose ten times larger, without the leftover melatonin the next day.
