## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- Intentions are translated into action roughly half the time.
- Correlational meta-analysis: intentions correlate strongly with later behaviour (r = 0.53, Sheeran 2002).
- Experimental meta-analysis: a medium-to-large change in intention produces a small-to-medium change in behaviour (d = 0.36, Webb & Sheeran 2006).
- The gap is mainly people who intend to act but don't ('inclined abstainers').
- Reasons: goals that are over-optimistic, hard, or set in a 'cold' state that ignores later urges; intentions driven by social pressure; habits; trouble getting started (forgetting, missed chances, procrastination), staying on track (not monitoring progress, competing goals, low mood, low willpower) and stopping well.
- Strategies: if-then plans (implementation intentions, d = 0.65 across 94 studies); progress monitoring (d = 0.40 on behaviour); others more tentative.

### Good Intentions Turn Into Action Only Half the Time

In a 2016 review, psychologists Paschal Sheeran and Thomas Webb brought together their own and others' research on why people so often fail to do what they intend.

They report that intentions become action only about half the time. Even when an intervention genuinely shifts what people intend, behaviour shifts much less. Most of the gap comes from people who want to change but don't, because they forget, miss the moment, procrastinate, lose track of progress, or get pulled back by habit and competing goals.

Two tools stand out: making specific if-then plans, and regularly checking progress.

**The main takeaway:** Wanting to change is only half the battle; specific plans and progress checks do much of the rest.
