## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- Participants decided whether to accept gambles with a 50/50 chance of winning or losing money, in a brain scanner.
- Activity in a broad set of areas, including dopamine regions and their targets, rose as potential gains increased.
- Potential losses were represented by decreasing activity in many of the same areas.
- Individual differences in loss aversion in behaviour were predicted by 'neural loss aversion' in regions including the ventral striatum and prefrontal cortex.

### Brain Reward Areas React More to Losses Than Gains

In a 2007 study at the University of California, Los Angeles in the USA, researchers scanned the brains of adult volunteers as they accepted or turned down coin-toss gambles to win or lose money.

Reward-related areas became more active as the possible win grew and less active as the possible loss grew, and the drop for losses was steeper. People whose brains reacted more steeply to losses were also the ones who turned down more gambles.

**The main takeaway:** The brain's own reward signals mark losses down more sharply than they mark gains up.
