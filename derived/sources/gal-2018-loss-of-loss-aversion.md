### Losses May Not Always Loom Larger Than Gains

In a 2018 review, consumer psychologists David Gal and Derek Rucker in the USA re-examined the evidence behind the popular idea that losses hurt more than equal gains please.

They argue the evidence does not support loss aversion as a general rule: in many situations losses and gains carry similar weight, and where losses do weigh more, it depends on the setting. They suggest the idea has outlasted its evidence partly because it is so familiar.

**The main takeaway:** Loss aversion may be real in some settings but not a universal rule of human choice.
