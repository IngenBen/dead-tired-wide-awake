## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- Exact replications of Schwabe & Wolf 2009 (stress before learning) and 2010 (stress after learning).
- Learning and food devaluation worked, and stress raised subjective and physiological stress measures.
- Both stressed and unstressed groups responded the same way to valued and devalued foods: the unstressed groups did not show goal-directed control, so the key test of a shift to habit could not be made.
- Other recent studies found stress shifted people towards habit only in some (for example people with low working memory or strong cortisol responses).
- Authors call for work on the conditions under which the effect appears.

### Stress-Habit Findings Fail to Reproduce

In a 2023 study led from Tilburg University in the Netherlands, researchers repeated two well-known German experiments exactly, each with about 90 adult men and women, after publicly registering their plans in advance.

As in the originals, some participants did the ice-water stress task and the rest did not, then all learned to press buttons for snacks and ate one snack until full.

The stress worked, but even the unstressed groups kept pressing for the snack they were full of. Because nobody showed the 'sensible' goal-directed pattern, the researchers could not tell whether stress had pushed anyone towards habit.

**The main takeaway:** The headline finding that stress pushes people into habit has not yet been reproduced, so it should be treated as plausible rather than proven.
