## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- Two groups did about 6.25 hours of task blocks over a working day: hard versions (frequent task switching, 3-back memory) or easy versions (rare switching, 1-back).
- Glutamate was measured by MR spectroscopy in the left lateral prefrontal cortex and primary visual cortex at the start, middle and end.
- At intervals, participants made choices between a small low-cost reward and €50 with a cost (a delay, uncertainty, or physical or mental effort).
- The hard group increasingly chose low-cost options for delay and for mental and physical effort, but not for uncertainty; response times did not speed up.
- The hard group's pupils dilated less during choices as the day went on, suggesting less deliberation.
- By the end of the day, prefrontal glutamate (relative to reference metabolites) was higher in the hard group than the easy group; no difference in visual cortex. The difference came mainly from a fall in the easy group.
- Glutamate diffusion in the prefrontal cortex rose in the hard group and correlated with the shift in choices (r = 0.43); glutamate concentration itself did not.
- Self-rated fatigue rose similarly in both groups.
- Authors propose that hard work causes glutamate to accumulate, making further prefrontal control more costly; correlational, and the model is a proof of concept.

### A Day of Hard Thinking Changes Brain Chemistry and Choices

In a 2022 study at the Paris Brain Institute in France, researchers had 40 young men and women spend a working day, about six hours, on computer tasks that were either mentally hard or easy.

At the start, middle and end of the day, a brain scanner measured glutamate, a chemical messenger, in the brain's planning region at the front and in the visual area at the back. Participants also made choices such as a small sum now or €50 later, or a small sum for no effort or €50 for hard work.

By the end of the day, the hard-work group more often took the easy, immediate option, and their pupils showed less deliberation. Their planning region held more glutamate than the easy group's, while the visual area showed no difference. Both groups said they felt about equally tired.

**The main takeaway:** A long day of hard mental work may leave a chemical build-up in the brain's planning region, and people then lean towards easy, immediate choices.
