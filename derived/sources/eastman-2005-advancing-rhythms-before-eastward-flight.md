## Abstract (close summary, not copied text)

Aim: a practical pre-flight treatment to move the body clock earlier before flying east, without putting it out of step with sleep. 26 healthy adults aged 22–36 had their sleep schedule moved 1 h or 2 h earlier per day for 3 days, with intermittent bright morning light (half-hour blocks of about 5000 lux alternating with dim light, over 3.5 hours). Melatonin onset moved earlier by a median 1.9 h (2 h/day group) and 1.4 h (1 h/day group); means 1.8 and 1.5 h, difference not significant. By day 3 the 2 h/day group's clocks were out of step with their sleep and they had trouble falling asleep; the 1 h/day group did not, though the 2 h/day group scored slightly better on symptoms. Side effects were small. The authors conclude a gradually advancing schedule with morning light can shift the clock before eastward travel, and that 2 h a day is too fast for the clock to keep up.

### Shift Your Clock Before Flying East, an Hour a Day

In a 2005 study at Rush University in Chicago, USA, researchers tried to shift people's body clocks before an eastward flight. 26 healthy young adults moved their sleep an hour or two earlier each day for three days and sat in front of bright light boxes on and off for a few hours after waking.

Both groups' clocks moved about an hour and a half earlier in three days. Moving two hours a day was too fast: the clock fell behind the new bedtime and people struggled to fall asleep. One hour a day kept clock and sleep in step.

**The main takeaway:** Before flying east, moving bedtime about an hour earlier a day with bright morning light can shift your clock ahead of time; going faster than the clock can follow just causes poor sleep.
