## Key content (summarised, not copied)

Review of how far and how long dopamine signals reach. Dopamine is described as a prototypical neuromodulator acting through slower G protein-coupled receptors: after release it diffuses to activate receptors on many target cells, unlike fast point-to-point synaptic transmission. The authors argue the system is more refined than the classic picture of diffuse "volume transmission": release happens at sparse, active-zone-like sites, receptors within about 1 micrometre of a release site respond, and synchronised firing across many dopamine cells creates overlapping signalling zones that reach further. Dopamine therefore sits between diffuse volume transmission and classic synaptic transmission.

### Dopamine Broadcasts to Many Cells, Not Just One

In a 2021 review from Harvard Medical School in the US, researchers pulled together what is known about how dopamine travels in the brain. Unlike most chemical messages, which pass from one nerve cell to the next across a tight gap, dopamine is released and spreads out to act on many nearby cells at once. That spread is more targeted than scientists once thought, but it is still a broadcast rather than a private message.

**The main takeaway:** Dopamine works as a broad signal that changes how whole groups of brain cells respond, which is why it is called a neuromodulator.
