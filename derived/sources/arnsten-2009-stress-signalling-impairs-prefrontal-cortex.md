### Stress Quickly Knocks Out the Brain's Planner

In a 2009 review at Yale University in the USA, neuroscientist Amy Arnsten summarised research, much of it in animals, on how stress affects the prefrontal cortex, the brain region behind planning and self-control.

She reports that this region is the most sensitive in the brain to stress. Even mild stress that feels out of one's control can quickly impair it, and long-running stress can change the shape of its nerve cells.

**The main takeaway:** Stress rapidly weakens the part of the brain needed for deliberate, planned behaviour.
