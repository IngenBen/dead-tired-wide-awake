## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- **Scope:** fixed-dose, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in adults, either healthy volunteers or people with primary insomnia; MEDLINE to March 2021 plus reference lists. Excluded: children, doses above 10 mg, melatonin used to shift the clock, circadian or other sleep disorders, shift work, illness. 26 trials (1987–2020), 1,689 observations. Most were small, single-night lab studies with EEG; doses 0.1–10 mg; duration 1–28 days; taken 0–3.5 h before the sleep episode. Zhdanova 2001 is among them.
- **Overall effect vs placebo:** sleep onset 9.3 min faster (95% CI 6.2–12.4 min; 25 trials, moderate heterogeneity, funnel-plot asymmetry suggesting possible publication bias, Egger p = 0.015). Total sleep 19.7 min longer (95% CI 12.4–27.0 min; 19 trials; no asymmetry).
- **Dose, sleep onset (Table 2, relative to 2 mg):** placebo 10.45 min slower than 2 mg; 1 mg 3.84 min slower; 3 mg 1.73 min faster; 4 mg 2.06 min faster; 5 mg 1.68 min faster (not significant). Converted to faster than placebo *(my arithmetic)*: 1 mg ≈ 6.6 min, 2 mg ≈ 10.5 min, 3 mg ≈ 12.2 min, 4 mg ≈ 12.5 min, 5 mg ≈ 12.1 min. The curve is not straight: it rises to a peak at about 4 mg and flattens, with no further benefit above. No value is given for 0.5 mg.
- **Dose, total sleep (Table 6, relative to 2 mg):** placebo 14.57 min less than 2 mg; 1 mg 5.33 min less; 3 mg 2.33 min more (significant); 4 mg 2.67 min more and 5 mg 2.02 min more (neither significant). Converted to more than placebo *(my arithmetic)*: 1 mg ≈ 9.2 min, 2 mg ≈ 14.6 min, 3 mg ≈ 16.9 min, 4 mg ≈ 17.2 min, 5 mg ≈ 16.6 min. Peak between 3 and 5 mg.
- **Dose in the meta-regression:** when timing and insomnia status were in the model, dose itself was not a significant predictor of sleep onset (p = 0.39) or total sleep (p = 0.62).
- **Timing, sleep onset (Table 5):** predicted gain vs placebo 3.5 min if taken at bedtime, 5.2 min at 30 min before, 6.1 min at 1 h, 13.5 min at 2 h, 19.6 min at 3 h, 20.0 min at 3.5 h. Taking it 2 or 3 h before the sleep episode was significantly better than at bedtime; 1 h before was not. Only two subsamples were dosed earlier than 3 h.
- **Worked cases from the model:** 2 mg 30 min before a 22:30 bedtime predicts almost no effect; 4 mg 3 h before predicts 8.6 min faster sleep onset; 2 mg 3 h before predicts 7.8 min. The authors conclude timing matters more than dose, recommend the lowest effective dose, and suggest up to 4 mg only when lower doses fail, after weighing costs and benefits. They also say going below 2 mg may not give the maximum reduction.
- **Who it helps:** the effect on sleep onset was smaller in people with insomnia than in healthy volunteers. Total sleep gains were larger the earlier in the day it was taken (relevant to daytime sleep, not bedtime).
- **Context:** the best predicted effect is under half the sleep-onset gain from CBT for insomnia (about 19 min in another meta-analysis).
- **Limitations stated:** healthy and insomnia samples only, so not generalisable to other sleep disorders; timing data only up to 3.5 h before sleep; possible publication bias; results are model predictions that need testing in trials.

### Melatonin Works Best Taken Earlier, Up To About 4 mg

In a 2024 analysis led from the University of Pisa in Italy, researchers pooled 26 trials comparing melatonin with a dummy pill in healthy adults and people with insomnia. Most were small, one-night lab studies with brain-wave recordings, using doses from 0.1 to 10 mg. The researchers modelled how the dose, and how long before bed it was taken, changed the effect.

Overall, melatonin got people to sleep about nine minutes sooner and added about 20 minutes of sleep. The benefit grew with dose up to about 3–4 mg, then flattened: in the model, 1 mg got people to sleep about seven minutes faster than a dummy pill and 3–4 mg about 12 minutes faster. But timing mattered more. Taken right at bedtime, melatonin barely helped; taken two to three hours before bed, the predicted effect was several times larger. Even at the right time, the gain was well under what talking therapy for insomnia achieves.

**The main takeaway:** As a sleep aid, melatonin's modest effect grows a little with dose up to about 4 mg, but taking it two to three hours before bed matters more than taking more.

- **Evidence:** Moderate (largest dose analysis available, but built on small short trials)
