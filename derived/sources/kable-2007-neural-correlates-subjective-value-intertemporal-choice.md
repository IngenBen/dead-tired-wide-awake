### One Brain System Discounts Future Rewards, Not Two

In a 2007 study at New York University in the USA, researchers scanned adult volunteers' brains while they chose between money now and larger amounts later, and worked out from each person's choices how steeply they discounted the future.

Activity in the brain's reward and valuation areas rose and fell with each person's own sense of what a delayed reward was worth. That fits a single valuation system that marks future rewards down, rather than one system for now competing with another for later.

**The main takeaway:** The brain marks down future rewards in a single valuation system, and how steeply it does so differs from person to person.
