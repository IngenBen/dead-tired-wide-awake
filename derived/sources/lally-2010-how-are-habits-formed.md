## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- 96 volunteers each chose an eating, drinking or activity behaviour to do daily in the same context (for example after breakfast) for 12 weeks.
- Each day they completed the self-report habit index and recorded whether they did the behaviour.
- 82 provided enough data; an automaticity curve fitted 62, well for 39. More consistent performance went with better fit.
- Time to reach 95% of each person's plateau in automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days.
- Missing one opportunity did not materially affect habit formation.
- With repetition in a consistent context, automaticity rises along a curve that levels off.

### New Habits Take Weeks to Months, Not 21 Days

In a study published in 2009 and 2010 at University College London in the UK, researchers asked 96 adult volunteers to pick one healthy eating, drinking or exercise habit and do it every day at the same point in their routine, such as after breakfast, for 12 weeks.

Every day they logged whether they had done it and rated how automatic it felt.

Automaticity climbed quickly at first and then levelled off. How long it took to get close to that ceiling varied hugely, from 18 days to an estimated 254. Missing the odd day made little difference.

**The main takeaway:** A new habit becomes automatic gradually, often over months, and an occasional missed day does not reset the clock.
