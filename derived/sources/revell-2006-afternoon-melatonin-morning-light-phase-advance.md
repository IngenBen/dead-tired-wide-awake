## Abstract (close summary, not copied text)

Question: can afternoon melatonin add to the clock-advancing effect of morning bright light and a gradually earlier sleep schedule? 44 healthy adults aged 19–45 had 3 days of waking 1 h earlier each morning, bright light on waking (four 30-minute pulses of about 5000 lux alternating with 30 minutes of room light), and afternoon melatonin (0.5 mg or 3.0 mg, timed for the largest advance) or placebo. Melatonin onset moved earlier by 2.5 h on 0.5 mg (n = 16) and 2.6 h on 3.0 mg (n = 13), against 1.7 h on placebo (n = 15); both doses beat placebo and did not differ from each other. No jet-lag-type symptoms. The authors conclude the combination moves the clock almost 1 h a day with very little misalignment, usable before eastward travel or for delayed sleep phase.

### Afternoon Melatonin Plus Morning Light: Nearly an Hour a Day

In a 2006 study at Rush University in Chicago, USA, researchers tried to move 44 healthy adults' body clocks earlier as fast as possible without causing jet-lag symptoms. For three days, everyone got up an hour earlier each morning and sat in bright light on and off after waking. In the afternoon they took 0.5 mg melatonin, 3 mg melatonin, or a dummy pill, without knowing which.

Light and the earlier schedule alone moved the clock about an hour and three-quarters in three days. Adding melatonin pushed that to about two and a half hours, and the small dose worked just as well as the large one. Nobody felt jet-lagged along the way.

**The main takeaway:** Getting up earlier with bright morning light, plus a small afternoon melatonin dose, moves the body clock nearly an hour a day, and 0.5 mg works as well as 3 mg.
