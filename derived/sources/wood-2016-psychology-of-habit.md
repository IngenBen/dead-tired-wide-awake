## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- People tend to repeat the same behaviours in recurring settings.
- Habits form as people pursue goals by repeating the same response in a given context.
- Habits and deliberate goal pursuit work together, but habits are the efficient, default mode of response.
- People tend to infer from how often they do something that they must have intended it.
- The review applies habit research to stress, addiction and the design of behaviour-change interventions.

### Habits Are the Brain's Efficient Default

In a 2016 review at the University of Southern California in the USA, psychologists Wendy Wood and Dennis Rünger summarised research on how habits form and how they interact with conscious goals.

They describe habits as forming when people repeat the same action in the same setting while pursuing a goal. Once formed, habits become the efficient default, and deliberate choice has to override them. People also tend to assume that what they do often must be what they meant to do.

**The main takeaway:** Repeated actions in the same setting become the default, and changing them means overriding that default.
