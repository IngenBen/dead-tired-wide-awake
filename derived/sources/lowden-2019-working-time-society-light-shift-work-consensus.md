## Summary (close summary, not copied text)

As part of a Working Time Society and International Commission on Occupational Health initiative, the authors reviewed field, human lab and animal research on using bright light to manage body-clock adjustment to shift work, and set out recommendations by type of roster. On slowly rotating night-shift rosters, bright light works best in the first three night shifts, and works better combined with orange glasses that block blue-green light on the commute home. Where full adjustment isn't the aim, the recommendation is less light at night and more natural daylight by day, also advised for day shifts, in line with how the body clock responds to light at different times.

### Bright Light at Work, Tinted Glasses on the Way Home

In a 2019 consensus statement, the Working Time Society and the International Commission on Occupational Health set out evidence-based advice on using light to help shift workers' body clocks. For rotating night shifts, bright light during the first three nights helps the clock adjust, especially if workers wear orange-tinted glasses to block blue-green light on the way home. Where adjusting fully isn't the goal, they advise keeping light lower at night and getting plenty of daylight during the day. The basis is a review of field studies, lab studies in people and animal research.

**The main takeaway:** For shift workers, managing light (bright at the right time, blocked on the way home) is the main tool for helping the body clock cope.
