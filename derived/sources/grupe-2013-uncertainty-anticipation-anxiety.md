### Uncertain Threats Fuel Anxiety

In a 2013 review at the University of Wisconsin–Madison in the USA, psychologists Daniel Grupe and Jack Nitschke looked at the brain science of anxiety through the lens of uncertainty.

They argue that not knowing whether, when or how a threat will arrive is what makes it hard to prepare for, and that this is the core of anxiety. They set out five mental processes that normally handle uncertain threats and suggest that anxiety disorders arise when these go awry.

**The main takeaway:** Uncertainty about what is coming is a central driver of anxiety.
