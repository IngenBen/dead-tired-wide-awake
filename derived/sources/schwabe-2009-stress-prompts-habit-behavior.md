## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- Stress group: hand in ice water for up to 3 minutes while videotaped and watched by a stranger (socially evaluated cold pressor test). Control: warm water, unobserved. Stress confirmed by ratings, blood pressure and saliva cortisol.
- Then everyone learned two actions, each earning a different food reward.
- One food was then 'devalued' by letting participants eat it until full.
- Tested without rewards: controls cut back on the action for the food they were full of (goal-directed); stressed participants kept doing both actions equally (habitual).
- Stressed participants also knew less about which action led to which food, and less awareness went with more habitual behaviour.
- Authors link this to stress effects on the prefrontal cortex, which carries many stress-hormone receptors.

### Stress Makes People Fall Back on Habit

In a 2009 study at Ruhr University Bochum in Germany, researchers gave 67 adult men and women either a stressful task or a harmless one before teaching them a simple computer game.

The stressed group held a hand in ice water for up to three minutes while being filmed and watched by a stranger; the others put a hand in warm water. Everyone then learned two button presses, each earning a different snack. Afterwards they ate one snack until they had had enough of it, then played again.

Unstressed people mostly stopped pressing for the snack they were sick of. Stressed people carried on pressing for both, as if on autopilot, and were also less clear about which button earned which snack.

**The main takeaway:** Under stress, people are more likely to repeat what they have learned to do, even when it no longer gets them what they want.
