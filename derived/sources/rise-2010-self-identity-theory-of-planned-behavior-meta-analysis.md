## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- (As reported in the 2021 review.) Self-identity added about 6% of explained variance in intentions beyond the theory of planned behaviour variables.
- Its independent contribution held, with total added variance about 9%, when past behaviour was also controlled.
- Self-identity and attitude were only modestly correlated (about 0.14), suggesting they are distinct.
- Critics (Fishbein & Ajzen 2010) argue self-identity measures partly capture past behaviour.

### Seeing a Habit as 'Who You Are' Predicts Intentions

In a 2010 analysis, psychologists Jostein Rise, Paschal Sheeran and Silje Hukkelberg pooled studies that asked people how far a behaviour, such as recycling or exercising, was part of who they were.

People who saw a behaviour as part of their identity were more likely to intend to do it, over and above how they felt about it, what others expected and what they had done before. The added effect was modest.

**The main takeaway:** How much a behaviour feels like 'you' adds a modest extra push towards intending to do it.
