## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- Adapted an established discounting method: participants chose between a smaller reward for an easier working-memory task (n-back) and a larger reward for a harder one.
- The reward people were willing to give up to avoid the harder task gives a measurable 'subjective cost' of cognitive effort.
- The cost was sensitive to task difficulty and reward size, and differed with age and with trait enjoyment of effortful thinking.
- The measure was validated against delay-discounting measures.

### People Will Pay to Avoid Harder Mental Work

In a 2013 study, researchers asked younger and older adults to choose between doing an easier memory task for less money or a harder one for more.

By adjusting the offers, they found the point at which each person was indifferent, which shows how much money the harder task 'cost' them. People gave up more money to avoid harder levels, and the price differed with age and with how much people enjoy demanding thinking.

**The main takeaway:** The cost of mental effort can be measured as the reward people will give up to avoid it, and it grows with difficulty.
