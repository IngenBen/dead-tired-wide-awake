## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- **Design:** randomised, counterbalanced crossover. 20 healthy young adults (13 women, 7 men; median age 18). After 5 minutes of quiet semi-reclined rest, 5 minutes of nose-only breathing (lips sealed, checked by observation) and 5 minutes of mouth-only breathing (soft nose clip), in random order, paced by metronome at each person's own breathing rate. Then light cycling (75 W) in each mode.
- **At rest:** "mean BP and diastolic BP were lower with nasal breathing" (diastolic about 68 vs 72 mmHg). Systolic pressure and heart rate did not differ. "HF contribution was higher and LF/HF was lower during nasal breathing" (high-frequency share about 59% vs 52%; a medium effect), read as a greater parasympathetic contribution. Breathing rate did not differ (15 vs 15 breaths a minute).
- **During cycling:** nearly all measures were similar; breathlessness ratings were lower with nose breathing.
- **Authors' conclusion:** modest, possibly clinically relevant benefits of nose breathing at rest, not during light exercise.
- **Stated limitations:** no measures of oxygen uptake, breath volume or exhaled CO2; small sample with no advance power calculation (some secondary analyses on 10–13 people); no correlation found between the blood-pressure and HRV changes, so the mechanism is speculative; healthy young adults only; long-term effects unknown.

### Nose Breathing at Rest Nudges Blood Pressure Down

In a 2023 study at Florida State University in the USA, researchers had 20 healthy young men and women sit quietly and breathe for five minutes only through the nose and five minutes only through the mouth, in random order.

During mouth breathing a soft clip closed the nose; during nose breathing they kept their lips sealed. A metronome kept them at their own natural breathing pace in both, so the only difference was the route the air took. Afterwards they did the same on an exercise bike at a light effort.

At rest, nose breathing lowered the lower (diastolic) blood pressure number by about 4 points and tilted heart-rate variability towards the body's calming side. Breathing pace and heart rate stayed the same. On the bike, the two made almost no difference, though nose breathing felt less breathless.

**The main takeaway:** Breathing through your nose at rest, at the same pace, seems to settle the body slightly more than breathing through your mouth.
