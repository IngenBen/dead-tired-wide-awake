## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- Study 1: 65 students tracked breakfast and newspaper reading for 10 weeks; during exam weeks, strong habits were performed more often, desirable and undesirable alike; weak habits did not change.
- Study 2: on days using the non-dominant hand (a depletion task), strongly habitual behaviours rose whether or not they fitted people's goals.
- Study 3: after a hard midterm, MBA students were more likely to pick their habitual snack, healthy or not.
- Study 4: an online depletion task increased habitual snack choice, partly through unthinking repetition.
- Study 5: people lower in trait self-control spent more study time on strongly habitual strategies.
- Limitations: student samples; exam weeks also bring time pressure and hunger; depletion tasks used in Studies 2 and 4.

### Under Pressure, People Do What They Usually Do

In a 2013 set of studies, researchers followed 65 university students for 10 weeks, asking them to record what they ate for breakfast and what they read in the newspaper.

During exam weeks, students did their strong habits even more than usual, whether that meant their usual cereal or their usual pastry. Things they did only sometimes didn't change. Further studies found the same pattern after a hard exam and on days when self-control was taxed.

**The main takeaway:** When people are stretched, they fall back on their strong habits, so good habits protect them and bad habits catch them.
