## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- One explanation for mental effort and declining performance is a physical resource that is used up over time.
- The authors propose instead that the brain's executive systems can only serve a few tasks at once, so using them has an opportunity cost: the next-best use they could be put to.
- The feeling of effort is the felt output of these cost-benefit calculations, and it motivates pulling back from the current task.
- They review evidence across psychology and neuroscience and set out ways to tell the models apart.

### Mental Effort Signals Missed Opportunities, Not Empty Fuel

In a 2013 theory paper at the University of Pennsylvania in the USA, psychologist Robert Kurzban and colleagues asked why hard mental tasks feel effortful and why performance slips over time.

They argue against the idea that a physical fuel runs low. Instead, the brain's planning systems can only do a few things at once, and the feeling of effort is the brain tallying what it is giving up by staying on the current task. That feeling then nudges a person to stop or switch.

**The main takeaway:** Feeling mentally drained may be the brain asking whether something better could be done, not a tank running dry.
