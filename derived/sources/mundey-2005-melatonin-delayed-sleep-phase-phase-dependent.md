## Abstract (close summary, not copied text)

13 people with delayed sleep phase syndrome were randomly assigned to 0.3 mg melatonin, 3.0 mg melatonin or placebo, taken between 1.5 and 6.5 hours before their dim-light melatonin onset for 4 weeks. Both doses advanced the timing of the body's own melatonin rhythm. The size of the advance correlated strongly with when the dose was taken, with earlier dosing more effective (r² = 0.94, P < .0001). The authors conclude that melatonin advances the body clock and sleep in this condition in a phase-dependent way. The abstract gives no separate figures for the two doses.

### For Night Owls, Melatonin Timing Mattered More Than Dose

In a 2005 study in the USA, researchers gave 13 people whose body clocks ran very late a small 0.3 mg dose, a larger 3 mg dose, or a dummy pill for four weeks, taken some hours before their natural melatonin rise. Their body-clock timing was measured before and after.

Both doses moved the clock earlier. What predicted how far it moved was how early in the evening the pill was taken: the earlier, the bigger the shift. The abstract doesn't report a difference between the two doses.

**The main takeaway:** For shifting a late body clock, getting the timing right matters more than taking a bigger dose.
