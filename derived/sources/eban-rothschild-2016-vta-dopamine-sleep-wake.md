## Abstract (summarised, not copied)

Dopamine cells in the ventral tegmental area (VTA) are involved in behaviours that need heightened arousal, but it was unknown whether they directly cause wakefulness. The researchers recorded these cells' activity in freely moving mice and found it changed with sleep–wake state. Using chemogenetic and optogenetic tools with sleep recordings, they showed the cells are needed for arousal: inhibiting them suppressed wakefulness even when salient stimuli were present (relating to mating, feeding and predation), and before sleep the mice built nests. Stimulating the cells started and maintained wakefulness and suppressed sleep and nesting. Different projections of these cells affected arousal differently.

### Brain's Reward Cells Switch Wakefulness On and Off in Mice

In a 2016 study at Stanford University in the US, researchers recorded and controlled the dopamine-producing cells of the brain's reward area in mice. When the cells were switched off, the mice fell asleep, even with food, a potential mate or the smell of a predator nearby, and first built careful nests. When the cells were switched on, sleeping mice woke and stayed awake.

**The main takeaway:** The same dopamine cells that drive motivation also directly keep the brain awake, at least in mice.
