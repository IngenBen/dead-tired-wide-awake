## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- Meta-analysis of 1,528 participants.
- Mental contrasting improved health behaviours, adjusted g = 0.28 at up to four weeks and g = 0.38 at up to three months (5 studies).
- Mental contrasting plus implementation intentions gave a similar effect (g = 0.28, 7 studies); in a small subset, adding if-then plans did not further strengthen effects.
- Described as a promising brief strategy with a small-to-moderate short-term effect.

### Mental Contrasting Modestly Improves Health Habits

In a 2019 analysis at the University of Derby in the UK, researchers pooled studies involving about 1,500 people that used mental contrasting to change health behaviours such as diet and exercise.

Picturing the desired outcome and then the obstacle standing in the way led to modest improvements in health behaviour, which held up to three months in the few studies that followed people that long.

**The main takeaway:** Weighing the goal against the obstacle, rather than just imagining success, gives a small but lasting nudge to healthier behaviour.
