## Findings (close summary, not copied text)

- 20 studies, 2,601 participants (mean ages 21.5–73.5).
- Behaviours included physical activity, diet, drinking water, flossing, vitamins and reducing sitting.
- Four studies reported time to habit: medians 59–66 days, means 106–154 days, individual range 4–335 days.
- Habit scores rose pre to post intervention (standardised mean difference 0.69).
- Frequency, timing, type of habit, personal choice, enjoyment and preparatory habits affected habit strength; morning and self-chosen habits tended to be stronger.
- 11 of the 20 studies were at high risk of bias.

### Health Habits Take About Two Months to Form

In a 2024 review at the University of South Australia in Adelaide, researchers pooled 20 studies involving about 2,600 adults who were trying to build health habits such as exercise, healthier eating or flossing.

In the studies that measured it, the typical time for a new habit to feel automatic was about two months, but some people got there in days and others took most of a year. Habits done in the morning, and habits people chose for themselves, tended to become stronger.

**The main takeaway:** Expect a new health habit to take around two months to become automatic, with big differences between people.
