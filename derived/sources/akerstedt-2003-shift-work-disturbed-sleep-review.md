## Abstract (close summary, not copied text)

Disturbed sleep is the most common health effect of shift work. The review covers how night, morning and afternoon shifts affect sleep and wakefulness, the mechanism of disruption in relation to the body clock, and the effect of rotation speed and direction. The worst acute symptoms are difficulty getting to sleep, shortened sleep, and sleepiness at work that continues into days off; changing shift patterns only partly helps. There is no clear sign that long-term shift work causes lasting sleep problems.

### Shift Work Shortens Sleep, and Rosters Only Partly Fix It

In a 2003 review, a Swedish researcher summarised how night, early-morning and afternoon shifts affect sleep. The most common problems are trouble getting to sleep, sleep that is cut short, and sleepiness at work that spills into days off. Changing how rosters rotate helps only partly. Once people stop shift work, there's no clear sign their sleep stays damaged.

**The main takeaway:** Shift work most often means shorter sleep and sleepiness on the job, and roster design alone can't fully solve it.
