## Key content (summarised, not copied)

283 elite Australian athletes (mean age 24) completed the Competitive Sports and Sleep questionnaire and the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index. 64.0% reported sleeping worse than usual at least once before an important competition in the previous 12 months. The most common problem was difficulty falling asleep (82.1%). Of those who gave reasons (n=176): thoughts about the competition 83.5%, nervousness 43.8%, unfamiliar surroundings 22.2%, noise 17.6%. Excitement was not offered as a reason. Most athletes had no strategy for it; team-sport athletes less often than individual athletes. The authors describe the problem as situational rather than a general sleep problem.

### Big Events Keep Elite Athletes Awake the Night Before

In a 2015 study in Australia, researchers surveyed 283 elite athletes about their sleep before important competitions. Nearly two in three had slept worse than usual before a big event at least once in the past year, mostly because they couldn't fall asleep. Most put it down to thinking about the competition; fewer said nerves.

**The main takeaway:** A big day ahead often stops people falling asleep, and it's usually the thinking about it, not just nerves.
