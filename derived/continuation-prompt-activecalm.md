## What was decided in the previous session

### The core decision

A concept called **Activating/Calming** has been elevated from a mid-book idea
(currently introduced in Ch18) to a Tier 1 through-line concept woven throughout
the entire book. It serves as the bridge between the biology section (Parts 2) and
the habits/program section (Part 4), and as the CEO's practical decision tool: every
behaviour is either an Activating or Calming signal sent to the executives.

### The agreed approach in detail

**Sleep Incorporated metaphor: kept entirely intact.**
The office language — executives clocking off, staying at their desks, refusing to
leave the building — is preserved. It is vivid, human, and does structural work
(hierarchy, downstream consequences) that signal language cannot replace.

**Activating/Calming: given its own terms, layered alongside the existing metaphor.**
Not a replacement. A second vocabulary that operates at a different level of
abstraction. Sleep Incorporated describes the system. Activating/Calming describes
how the CEO manages it.

**Introduction sequence:**
- End of Ch4: The concept (not yet the named terms) is established as the natural
  completion of the CEO metaphor. You're the CEO. Your behaviour speaks to your
  executives. It is either Active or Calm in nature. Terms not yet formally named.
- Ch5: First formal use of the terms, attached to CAFFEINE (not adenosine). "Every
  afternoon coffee is an Activating signal to the Wake Executives." Caffeine is a
  clean fit. Adenosine itself does not map to the Activating/Calming axis (it is a
  pressure accumulator, a different axis entirely) — so appends in Ch5 attach to
  the habit, not the hormone.
- Chs 6–8: Appends at key moments make the causal chain explicit — your behaviour
  → executive consequence — using the now-established vocabulary. Cortisol (Ch7)
  is the clearest fit and the point where the connection between term and mechanism
  is strongest.
- Ch9 (bridge): Formally states the concept as the gateway to the program.
  "Everything you do is either an Activating or Calming signal. This is how you
  speak to your executives." The reader arrives here through recognition, not
  instruction, because the vocabulary has already been building.
- Chs 10–24 (habit chapters): Appends become lighter. Reader should be doing the
  translation themselves by this point.

**Chapter order: not changed.** The concern about adenosine (Ch5) not fitting the
frame was resolved by attaching the terms to caffeine behaviour, not the hormone.
Reordering was solving a problem the append approach itself dissolves.

### Tier 2 fold-ins

Three Tier 2 concepts dissolve entirely into the established Activating/Calming
vocabulary:

- **Activating System / Calming System** (currently introduced in Ch18 as new named
  terms for sympathetic/parasympathetic): fully folds. Ch18 now extends existing
  vocabulary to the nervous system level rather than introducing a new concept.
- **Calm-good vs Hyped-good** (Ch8, Ch15, Ch21): fully folds. Expressible entirely
  in existing terms — educational evening content is still an Activating signal
  regardless of how it feels.
- **Top-down / Bottom-up** (Ch18): fully folds. Meditation works by sending a
  Calming signal from body to brain. The vocabulary is already there.

Two Tier 2 concepts remain:
- **Sleep Window**: stays as a named observable phenomenon. Its explanation is now
  carried by Activating/Calming (too many Activating signals into the evening close
  the window) but the concept itself earns its place.
- **Feedback Loop / 3-Layer Tracker**: stays unchanged. Practical guidance, not a
  conceptual framework.

## The revised Tier 1 list (in order of prominence)

1. **Sleep Incorporated** — the organising metaphor. CEO, executives, managers,
   workers. Everything sits inside or alongside this frame.
2. **Activating/Calming** — the through-line and practical decision tool. The bridge
   between biology and habits. What the reader uses in daily life, including in
   situations the 16 habits don't explicitly cover.
3. **The 4 Executives** — the science backbone. Sleep Executives (Adenosine,
   Melatonin) and Wake Executives (Cortisol, Dopamine), one chapter each. Without
   these, Activating/Calming has no biology to point to.
4. **The 6 Phases** — the program skeleton. Every habit chapter lives inside a phase.
5. **The 3 Categories** — body clock / nervous system / remove disrupters. The
   logical layer between the executives and the phases.
6. **The Fitness Analogy** — the foundational premise. No single fix, multi-input
   system, needs a foundation. Entry gate rather than through-line.
7. **The 16 Habits** — the deliverable. More a count than a concept.

The key relationship: Sleep Incorporated and Activating/Calming are complementary
at different levels. Sleep Inc = understanding the system. Activating/Calming =
managing it. Together they cover both WHY and WHAT TO DO — the gap the book
currently has between its biology and its program.

## What to discuss next

Examine the revised Tier 1 list for remaining complexity. Specifically:

- With Activating/Calming now doing significant organising work, do the 3 Categories
  still need to exist as a named concept, or are they now redundant?
- Does the Fitness Analogy earn its place as a Tier 1 concept, or is it a framing
  device that belongs in a lower tier?
- Is there anything in the current Tier 1 list that overlaps, creates unnecessary
  cognitive load for the reader, or could be folded now that Activating/Calming is
  carrying more weight?
- Are the 6 Phases and 16 Habits both needed as named concepts, or does one make
  the other implicit?

The working principle throughout: a concept earns its place if removing it would
require the reader to hold something more complex. If a simpler frame already covers
it, it goes.
