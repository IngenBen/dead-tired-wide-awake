## Recommended funnel — hybrid (info → call)

Public post earns a low-friction **comment**. Everything that could put someone off happens
privately in DM, where drop-off is visible and recoverable. Three DM touches, escalating
commitment:

1. Thank them + send a short written explainer + 2–3 qualifying questions.
2. For the warm ones, invite to a cameras-off group call to walk through it and meet others.
3. Send the first-session link to everyone who joins / asks.

### Why the hybrid (drop-off best guesses, per 100 commenters — estimates, not data)

| | Call first | Info only | **Hybrid (info→call)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clear first barrier | ~40 | ~60 read | ~60 read |
| Commitment step | ~33 sign up | ~30 register | ~38 say yes to call |
| Attend first session | ~25–30 | ~18–22 | **~27–32** |
| Group cohesion | High | Low | High |
| Where you lose people | Early, visible | Middle, silent | Spread, visible |

Easy step first (shy/curious don't bounce off a live call before they understand the offer),
bonding step second (commitment is a human, two-way moment, not an ignorable link). Losses
are visible and recoverable. Cost: 3 touches/person — trivial at experiment scale.

**Format note:** "webinar" reads as salesy/broadcast — avoid. **Decided: Zoom/Meet link**
(waiting room + recording, no Facebook-friend requirement — anyone can join from the link
regardless of connection status). Cameras off / muted is fine to state as the norm. Facebook
Live and native Messenger group calls ruled out (Live is broadcast not conversation; native
Messenger calls require being FB friends with each attendee, which most commenters won't be).

## Post comparison — predicted response (Post 1 vs Post 2)

**Verdict: run Post 1 ("The post (final)") as primary.** Strongest hook in either draft —
concrete quantified before/after ("4–5 hours broken → 7+ hours decent") lands in line one,
which is the highest-converting pattern for this kind of copy. Post 2's hook (meta-observation
on the group's own "what's the one thing" question) is a good pattern-interrupt but more
cerebral — it comments on the audience before it promises them anything.

- **Relatability:** Post 1's suffering beat is visceral and universal ("mind wouldn't shut
  off," "running a race in lead boots") — recognition-driven, which is what gets a comment in
  a support group. Post 2's equivalent ("tried dozens of 'one things' over two decades") is
  true but more abstract/cerebral.
- **Trust/positioning:** Post 1 stays peer-to-peer throughout, no project framing. Post 2
  introduces "I'm writing a book about it" — adds authority but risks reading as
  self-promotion in a support-group context (exactly what triggers skepticism or mod removal).
  Real risk to comment volume, not just tone.
- **Where Post 2 might win:** hard numbers on structure (12 habits: 3/3/6) vs Post 1's vaguer
  "three things" — more credible to a skeptical/analytical reader, possibly a higher-trust
  but smaller subset of commenters.
- **Recommendation:** Post 1 primary; hold Post 2 as a fallback/second-week test if response
  is soft, since its angle may land better with a more analytical audience.

**Flag — posting-notes mismatch found:** the posting note directly under "The post (final)"
heading ("CTA word is 'hear more'... Book mention adds credibility") actually describes Post
2's content, not the text it's attached to — looks like a leftover from reordering when "The
post (final)" was promoted above Post 1/Post 2. The note under the "Post 1" heading (open
CTA, no book mention) correctly matches "The post (final)" text. Don't apply the wrong
posting instructions to the wrong post when publishing.

### Post 3 added — skeptic-perspective read (2026-07-22)

**Verdict unchanged: Post 1 ("The post (final)") stays primary.** Read against a sleep-deprived,
*skeptical* persona specifically (not just a tired one), two things matter most: cognitive load
and evidence vs. assertion.

- **Show vs. tell:** Post 1 opens with a data point ("4–5 hours broken → 7+ hours decent");
  Post 3 opens with a claim ("I found something that worked for me"). A skeptical reader
  trusts the data point faster and with less effort.
- **Numbered roadmap risk:** Post 3's 3-step plan is meant to read as transparency, but to an
  already-suspicious reader a numbered plan from a stranger can itself pattern-match to a
  pitch deck — "here's my plan, no hiding it" is a recognisable persuasion move, which a
  skeptic may clock and discount rather than credit.
- **Where Post 3 wins:** its "I don't believe magic cures exist" line and the fitness metaphor
  are the strongest skepticism-defusing content of the three — genuinely stronger than Post
  1's brisk "no catch, I'm just curious." But that argument only pays off if the reader has
  the bandwidth to read the whole post, which is the population least likely to have it.
- **Post 2 confirmed weakest for this persona too:** "I'm writing a book about it" is a harder
  stop for an already-suspicious reader than an average one.
- **Recommendation:** keep Post 1 primary. Don't shelve Post 3 — reuse its reasoning
  (not-one-thing, fitness metaphor, no-catch-no-upsells) as a second touch rather than the
  lead: either a second-week test post (same fallback logic already applied to Post 2), or
  folded into DM 1, where the reader has already opted in and has more patience for an
  argument.

## Alternative funnel under consideration — DM link → landing page video → fork

Proposed: DM contains a link to a landing page; landing page carries a video explainer with
full detail; after the video, two CTAs branch the audience — "if you've decided, leave your
email" / "if you want to know more, join the group call to ask questions."

**Engagement/drop-out comparison vs. the hybrid above:**

- **Extra hop raises the first barrier.** Reading a DM costs nothing; clicking out to a
  landing page is a bigger ask, especially from a tired, low-energy insomnia audience at
  night. Expect more loss at this step than the hybrid's in-app read (~60/100 estimate).
- **Video trades attention for trust.** A personal video out-trusts text (voice, tone, face
  fit the fellow-sufferer angle), but demands more attention than three lines of DM text —
  some will bail rather than watch. Likely a wash on volume, but a quality shift: those who
  finish the video are more warmed-up than DM-text skimmers.
- **No qualifying step before commitment.** The hybrid's DM 1 did double duty — explainer +
  3 quick questions (duration / cause / doctor involvement) — screening acute/medical cases
  out before they enter the funnel. This design has no equivalent gate before someone leaves
  an email or joins the call. Fix: fold a "does this sound like you?" line into the video, or
  a 1–2 question form before the email field.
- **Email/call fork is the strongest piece.** Splitting "decided" from "still deciding" beats
  the hybrid's single mandatory gate — ready people don't wait on a scheduled call, and
  fence-sitters aren't forced to commit before questions are answered. Should raise total
  completions versus one forced path.
- **Net read:** trades some of the hybrid's personal touch and built-in screening for scale
  and fewer manual touches from Ben. Likely similar or slightly lower total attendance than
  the hybrid unless the video closes the trust gap the missing DM back-and-forth would
  otherwise leave.
- **Link safety note:** FB is generally more permissive of external links inside private DMs
  than in public posts, but use the real domain (not a shortener) to avoid spam flags.
