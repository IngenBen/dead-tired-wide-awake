# Reader Feedback Brief — Sleep Incorporated (manuscript)

*A ready-to-send pack for collecting useful feedback. Pick the question sheet that matches each
reader. Send the same manuscript version to a whole round; don't tweak mid-round.*

---

## Who to send to (aim for 5–8 engaged readers, not 50 polite ones)

- **Target readers (priority).** Chronic poor sleepers who are busy and have tried-and-failed
  before — stressed parents, shift workers, "I've read everything" skeptics. Get a range: one
  mid-struggle, one cynical. Skip good sleepers; they're not the audience and will mislead you.
- **A "do-it" cohort (2–3 people).** Target readers who'll actually *run* the program for a few
  weeks. For a how-to book this is the strongest signal — and it feeds the app/community.
- **A sleep/medical professional.** Sleep physician or scientist, ideally plus a CBT-i/ACT-i
  psychologist or GP. Checks accuracy, safety, and where citations are needed.
- **One craft reader.** Reads memoir/practical non-fiction; speaks to voice, pacing, structure.

---

## Cover note (paste into the email; adjust to taste)

> Thanks for doing this — it genuinely helps.
>
> A few ground rules so your time isn't wasted: **praise doesn't help me — tell me where it
> dragged, confused you, or lost you.** Read it the way a real reader would (a sitting or two), and
> please jot the page where you stopped, skimmed, or felt like putting it down — that's the single
> most useful thing you can give me.
>
> Don't worry about fixing anything or polishing wording; just tell me your honest experience and
> where it happened. I've put a handful of questions at the end — answer the ones you have a view
> on. Happy to grab 30 minutes afterward if you're up for it.
>
> One version only, so we're all reacting to the same draft. Thanks again.

---

## Question sheet A — Target readers

1. Where did you see yourself in the opening chapters — and where did you *not*?
2. Where did you lose momentum or feel like putting it down? (Page/chapter, please.)
3. After reading, what's the first thing you'd actually *do*?
4. Did anything feel like it promised too much, or too little?
5. The "Sleep Incorporated / CEO / four executives" idea — did it help you understand things, feel
   gimmicky, or both?
6. Did the app/community mention feel helpful, or like a sales pitch?
7. In one sentence: what's the book about?
8. Anything you didn't believe, or that didn't apply to you?

## Question sheet B — "Do-it" cohort (after a few weeks)

1. Which habit did you start with? Did it stick? Where did you get stuck?
2. Did the program feel doable inside a normal busy week, or like too much?
3. Did you hit the "undeniable shift" the book promises — and if so, roughly when?
4. Which chapter/instruction was hardest to actually follow (not just read)?
5. Did the tracker/app help, get in the way, or go unused?

## Question sheet C — Sleep/medical professional

1. Anything inaccurate or overstated? (The science is deliberately simplified — flag where that
   tips into wrong.)
2. Anywhere a reader could come to harm — supplement dosages, alcohol, stopping medication,
   the menopause/HRT passage?
3. Are the "see your doctor" boundaries in the right places, and are any missing?
4. Which claims most need a citation before this goes public?
5. Anything you'd expect a sleep professional to push back on publicly?

## Question sheet D — Craft reader

1. Does the voice stay consistent, and does it ever tip into generic self-help?
2. Where does the pace sag or a chapter run long? (Watch the denser ones.)
3. Does the structure hold — memoir → science → program → habits → close?
4. Does the ending land?
5. What would make you recommend it to a friend — or not?

---

## How to run it

- **Stagger and target.** Don't mass-email the PDF with "thoughts?" — that yields vague praise.
- **Track drop-off.** The book is ordered to manage overwhelm (meditation is held back to Ch 20 on
  purpose). If readers bail before then, you need to know where and why.
- **Talk to 2–3 of them.** A 30-minute conversation reveals more than written notes — especially
  where they *hesitate*.
- **Look for patterns.** Act on what 3+ independent readers flag. Be skeptical of single strong
  opinions. Treat any fix they suggest as a symptom, not an instruction.
- **Don't defend.** In the moment, just collect. Argue with the manuscript later, not the reader.
